\section{From Classical to Quantum: Why Extend?}

In \cref{chap:EquivariantQuantumPositivity} (see \cite{Mihalcea}), Mihalcea proved the positivity of structure constants for two Schubert classes in equivariant quantum cohomology:

\[
\sigma(u) \circ \sigma(v) = \sum_{d} \sum_{w} q^d c_{u,v}^{w,d} \sigma(w)
\]

where the coefficients $c_{u,v}^{w,d} \in \Lambda = \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \ldots, x_r]$ are polynomials with nonnegative coefficients.

The natural next question: \textbf{Can we establish a similar positivity result for double Schubert polynomials in the quantum setting?}

\section{等变量子上同调：定义与引理}

为了建立量子双重 Schubert 多项式的 positivity 理论，我们首先需要严格定义等变量子上同调的结构。本节的符号遵循 \cref{chap:TriplePositivity}（Chapter 1）的约定。

记 $G = \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$，$B$ 为标准 Borel 子群，$T$ 为极大环面。设 $P \supset B$ 为包含 $B$ 的抛物子群，$W = S_n$ 为 $G$ 的 Weyl 群，$W_P \subset W$ 为对应的 Weyl 子群。记 $W^P \subset W$ 为 $W/W_P$ 的最短长度代表元集合 \footnote{最短代表元的定义见 \cref{def:ShortestLengthRepresentatives}（Chapter 1）。}，即每个陪集 $wW_P$ 中唯一的长度最短元素。

记 $\Lambda = H_T^*(\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{t}) = \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \ldots, x_r]$，其中 $x_i$ 对应负单根 $-\alpha_i$（following Chapter 1's convention）。\footnote{等变量子上同调类的几何定义见 \cref{sec:EquivariantCohomologyClass}（Chapter 1）。}

以下引理建立了等变量子上同调乘法结构的有限性，这是定义中的求和能够良定义的基础。

\begin{Lemma}[有限性引理]
\label{Lem:EQCFiniteSum}
设 $u, v \in W^P$ 为最短代表元。则在等变量子上同调乘法
\[
\sigma(u) \circ \sigma(v) = \sum_{d} \sum_{w \in W^P} q^d c_{u,v}^{w,d} \sigma(w)
\]
中，对每个固定的多重次数 $d = (d_1, \ldots, d_r)$，只有有限多个 $w \in W^P$ 使得 $c_{u,v}^{w,d} \neq 0$。
\end{Lemma}

\begin{Proof}
根据 Mihalcea \cite[Section 2]{Mihalcea}，等变量子 Littlewood-Richardson 系数 $c_{u,v}^{w,d}$ 由旗流形 $G/P$ 上的广义 Gromov-Witten 不变量定义：
\[
c_{u,v}^{w,d} = \langle [\mathcal{X}(u)], [\mathcal{X}(v)], [\mathcal{X}(w)] \rangle_{0,3,d}.
\]
这些不变量计数满足特定条件的稳定映射个数。由 Schubert 胞腔的维数公式可知，只有当
\[
\deg \mathcal{X}(u) + \deg \mathcal{X}(v) + \deg \mathcal{X}(w) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(G/P) + \sum_{i=1}^r d_i \cdot \deg(q_i)
\]
时，不变量才可能非零。由于 $\deg \mathcal{X}(w) = 2\ell(w)$（$\ell(w)$ 为 Weyl 群长度），而 $\ell(w)$ 对 $w \in W^P$ 有上界 $\ell(w_0^P)$（最长元素的长度），故满足此等式的 $w$ 只有有限多个。\qedhere
\end{Proof}

\begin{Definition}[\textbf{等变量子上同调}]
\label{def:EquivariantQuantumCohomology}
\textbf{等变量子上同调}（equivariant quantum cohomology）$QH_T^*(X)$ 是一个分次 $\Lambda[q]$-代数，其 $\Lambda[q]$-基底为 $\{\sigma(w) \mid w \in W^P\}$。乘法 $\circ$ 定义为：
\begin{equation}
\sigma(u) \circ \sigma(v) = \sum_{d} \sum_{w \in W^P} q^d c_{u,v}^{w,d} \sigma(w),\footnote{问：$\sigma(u) \circ \sigma(v) = \sum_{d} \sum_{w \in W^P} q^d c_{u,v}^{w,d} \sigma(w)$ 这个公式中，这两个求和分别是对什么求和的？见附录 \cref{sec:qa-eqc-summations}。}
\label{eq:EQCDefinition}
\end{equation}
其中 $c_{u,v}^{w,d}$ 称为\textbf{等变量子 Littlewood-Richardson 系数}（equivariant quantum LR coefficient），是 $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \ldots, x_r]$ 中的非负系数多项式。\footnote{问：在 \cref{def:EquivariantQuantumCohomology} 中提到：$QH_T^*(X)$ 是一个分次 $\Lambda[q]$-代数。什么是分次 $\Lambda[q]$-代数？其中的 $q$ 变量代表什么？见附录 \cref{sec:graded-Lambda-q-algebra}。}
\end{Definition}

\textbf{关键性质}（following Kim \cite{Kim2000}）：$c_{u,v}^{w,d} \in \Lambda = \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \ldots, x_r]$ 是齐次多项式，次数为
\[
\deg c_{u,v}^{w,d} = c(u) + c(v) - c(w) - \sum_{i=1}^r d_i \cdot \deg(q_i),
\]
其中 $c(w) = \ell(w)$ 是 $w$ 的 Coxeter 长度。\footnote{问：次数公式中的求和指标 $i$ 的范围是什么？见附录 \cref{sec:qa-degree-summation-range}。}

\textbf{与经典情形的关系}：等变量子上同调是以下两者的共同推广（见 \cite[Proposition 3.1]{Kim2000}）：
\begin{itemize}
\item 当 $d = 0$ 时，退化为等变上同调（equivariant cohomology）$H_T^*(X)$；
\item 当所有 $x_i = 0$ 时，退化为普通量子上同调（quantum cohomology）$QH^*(X)$。
\end{itemize}

\section{The Quantum Double Schubert Positivity Conjecture}

\begin{Remark}[Naming Clarification]
The ``Quantum Double Schubert Positivity Conjecture'' is precisely the \textbf{Graham Positivity of Triple Quantum Schubert Calculus} as studied by Gao-Xiong in \cite{GX2025}. Here ``double'' refers to the fact that each factor $\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$ is a \emph{double Schubert polynomial} — a Schubert polynomial in two sets of variables $(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$, where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathbf{x}$ parametrizes the base flag manifold,
\item $\mathbf{y}$ parametrizes the equivariant displacement (or the second set of Schubert conditions).
\end{itemize}
When multiplying two such polynomials $\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})$, we obtain coefficients $c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}; q)$ that are polynomials in the differences $y_i - z_j$ (the relative displacements between the two Schubert conditions) and the quantum parameters $q_k$.

The word ``Triple'' refers to the three Schubert polynomials appearing in the product formula:
\[
\underbrace{\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})}_{\text{1st}} \cdot \underbrace{\mathfrak{S}_v^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})}_{\text{2nd}} = \sum_w \underbrace{c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}; q)}_{\text{coefficients}} \cdot \underbrace{\mathfrak{S}_w^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})}_{\text{3rd}}.
\]
Thus three polynomials are involved: two on the left-hand side (with parameters $\mathbf{y}$ and $\mathbf{z}$ respectively) and one on the right-hand side (with parameter $\mathbf{y}$). The Graham positivity phenomenon states that the coefficients $c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}; q)$ lie in $\mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k]$.
\end{Remark}

\begin{Conjecture}[Quantum Double Schubert Positivity]
\label{conj:QuantumDoublePositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_{\infty}$, the product of quantum double Schubert polynomials expands as:

\[
\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}; q) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_w^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})
\]

The coefficients satisfy:
\[
c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}; q) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k]_{i,j \geq 1, k \geq 1}
\]
namely, the coefficients are polynomials in $y_i - z_j$ and $q_k$ with nonnegative integer coefficients.
\end{Conjecture}

\textbf{Relationship with proved cases}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Double classical case} (Graham, 2001): For $q = 0$, the coefficients $c_{u,v}^w(y;z)$ are nonnegative polynomials in $t_i - y_j$.
\item \textbf{Equivariant quantum case} (\cite{Mihalcea}): For two Schubert classes, $c_{u,v}^{w,d}$ are nonnegative polynomials in $x_i$.
\item \textbf{Quantum double case} (this conjecture): The unified setting with both $y_i - z_j$ parameters and quantum corrections $q_k$ remains open.
\end{enumerate}

The two key conjectures in this framework are:

\begin{Theorem}[Classical Double Schubert Positivity (Gao-Xiong, 2025) {\cite[Theorem 1.1]{GX2025}}]
\label{thm:ClassicalDoublePositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_{\infty}$, the product of double Schubert polynomials satisfies:
\[
\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})
\]
with coefficients $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j]_{i,j \geq 1}$.
\end{Theorem}

\begin{Proof}
The proof follows the strategy of Gao--Xiong \cite[Section 2]{GX2025}\footnote{详见\cref{sec:GX2025-Section2}（\cite[Section 2]{GX2025}）}，which extends Graham's positivity framework \cite{Gr}\footnote{详见\cref{def:GrahamPositivity}（\cite[Section 2.2]{Gr}）} to the double Schubert setting.

\subsection{Step 1: Geometric set-up}
\label{sec:GX2025-Section2}
Let $G = \mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$, and let $B$, $B^-$, $T$ denote the standard Borel subgroup, opposite Borel subgroup, and maximal torus, respectively. Consider the special involution
\[
\tau \in S_{2n}, \qquad \tau(i) = n + i, \quad \tau(n + i) = i \quad (1 \leq i \leq n).
\]
This element plays a key role in relating the two parameter sets $\mathbf{y}$ and $\mathbf{z}$.

For $w \in S_\infty$, denote the opposite Schubert variety by $X_w^\circ = B^- w B / B$ and its closure by $X_w = \overline{B^- w B / B}$. The $T$-equivariant cohomology class of $X_w$ is represented by the double Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$\footnote{双Schubert多项式定义见\cref{def:DoubleSchubertPolynomial}}；我们 refer to \cite[Section 1.3]{GX2025} for the precise conventions.

\subsection{Step 2: Normally transversal intersection}
The following geometric lemma is the engine of the proof. It is proved in \cite[Lemma 2.5]{GX2025} using the action of the opposite unipotent group $N^- = B^- \cap \tau B^- \tau^{-1}$ on $G/B$.

\begin{Lemma}[{\cite[Lemma 2.5]{GX2025}}]
\label{Lem:NormalTransversalIntersection}
The intersection
\[
\tau \cdot X_u \cap X_v
\]
is \emph{normally transversal} at every point of the scheme-theoretic intersection.
\end{Lemma}

Here $\tau \cdot X_u$ denotes the left translate of $X_u$ by $\tau$. The geometric meaning of normal transversality is that the intersection product in $T$-equivariant cohomology is given by the cup product of the corresponding characteristic classes; see \cite[Section 16.5]{AndersonFulton} for the general formalism.

\subsection{Step 3: Application of Graham positivity}
The key input is Graham's refined positivity theorem \cite{Gr}\footnote{详见\cref{th:GrahamPositivityRefined}（\cite[Corollary 2.4]{GX2025}）}，which computes the $T$-equivariant cohomology expansion coefficients in terms of the moment graph of $G/B$.

\begin{Theorem}[Graham positivity, {\cite[Corollary 2.4]{GX2025}}]
\label{th:GrahamPositivityRefined}
Let $w \in S_n$ and let $I(w) = \{\alpha \in \Delta^+ \mid w(\alpha) \in \Delta^-\}$ be the set of positive roots sent to negative roots by $w$. Then
\[
[X_w]_T = \sum_{v \leq w} c_{v,w} \cdot [\overline{B^- v B / B}]_T, \qquad c_{v,w} \in \mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(w)}.
\]
\end{Theorem}

Applying \cref{th:GrahamPositivityRefined} to the normally transversal intersection $\tau X_u \cap X_v$ and using the projection formula, we obtain
\[
[\tau X_u \cap X_v]_T = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}) \cdot [X_w]_T,
\]
where the coefficients $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z})$ are the structure constants of the double Schubert polynomial multiplication.

Since the intersection $\tau X_u \cap X_v$ is normally transversal, it is in particular \emph{compatibly $N^-(\tau)$-split} in the sense of \cite[Section 19.3]{AndersonFulton}, where $N^-(\tau) = N^- \cap \tau N^- \tau^{-1}$. By \cite[Corollary 2.4]{GX2025}, the coefficients $c_{u,v}^w$ therefore lie in
\[
\mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(\tau)}.
\]

\subsection{Step 4: Computing the root set}
It remains to identify the set $I(\tau)$. Since $\tau$ swaps the first $n$ indices with the last $n$ indices, the roots sent to negative roots are precisely
\[
I(\tau) = \{\, y_j - z_i \mid 1 \leq i, j \leq n \,\},
\]
where $y_1, \ldots, y_n$ are the weights of the first set of variables and $z_1, \ldots, z_n$ are the weights of the second set. Consequently
\[
c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_j - z_i \mid 1 \leq i,j \leq n]_{i,j \geq 1},
\]
which proves \cref{thm:ClassicalDoublePositivity} and hence \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}.

Finally, by the stability of Schubert polynomials \cite[Section 2.4]{Mihalcea}, the finite-$n$ statement extends to all $u, v, w \in S_\infty$ without modification. \qedhere
\end{Proof}

\section{Kirillov's Conjecture on Skew Divided Difference Operators}

A striking application of \cref{thm:ClassicalDoublePositivity} is the resolution of a conjecture of Kirillov concerning skew divided difference operators.

\begin{Corollary}[Kirillov's Conjecture, {\cite[Corollary 1.2]{GX2025}}]
\label{cor:KirillovConjecture}
For $u, v, w \in S_n$, the skew divided difference operator $\partial_{w / v}$ applied to the Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x})$ yields:
\[
\partial_{w / v} \mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[\mathbf{x}].
\]
\end{Corollary}

\begin{Proof}
The key identity from \cite[Section 2, p. 5]{GX2025} (or equivalently \cite[Proposition 6.4]{FGL2025}) states:
\[
\partial_{w / v} \mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) = c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{x}).
\]
Setting $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$ in \cref{thm:ClassicalDoublePositivity}, we obtain:
\[
\partial_{w / v} \mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}) = c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[\mathbf{x}].
\]
\qedhere
\end{Proof}

\begin{Remark}
The skew divided difference operators $\partial_{w / v}$, introduced by Macdonald \cite{macdonald1991}, provide an algebraic method for computing Schubert structure constants. The positivity result in \cref{cor:KirillovConjecture} demonstrates that these operators always produce polynomials with nonnegative coefficients when applied to Schubert polynomials—a phenomenon first conjectured by Kirillov \cite{kirillov2007} and now proved by Gao--Xiong.
\end{Remark}

\section{Refined Graham Positivity Theorem}

The proof of \cref{thm:ClassicalDoublePositivity} relies on a refined version of Graham's positivity theorem, which we now state in full.

\begin{Theorem}[Refined Graham Positivity, {\cite[Theorem 2.3]{GX2025}}]
\label{th:RefinedGrahamPositivity}
Let $B^-$ act on a non-singular variety $X$, and let $Y$ be a $B^-(w)$-invariant effective cycle in $X$. Then there exist $B^-$-invariant effective cycles $Z_1, \ldots, Z_m$ such that
\[
[Y]_T \in \sum_{i=1}^m \mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(w)} \cdot [Z_i]_T
\]
in $H_T^*(X)$.
\end{Theorem}

Here $I(w) = \{\alpha \in \Phi^+ \mid w^{-1}\alpha \in \Phi^-\}$ is the (left) inversion set of $w$, and $B^-(w) = T \cdot N^-(w)$ where $N^-(w) = N^- \cap wN^-w^{-1}$.

\begin{Remark}
When $w = w_0$ (the longest element), $B^-(w_0) = T$ and \cref{th:RefinedGrahamPositivity} recovers Graham's original positivity theorem \cite[Theorem 3.2]{Gr}. The refined version yields stronger results when $B^-(w)$ is larger than $T$, as the root set is restricted to $I(w)$ rather than all positive roots.
\end{Remark}

\section{Grothendieck Polynomials: Definition}

Grothendieck polynomials are $K$-theoretic generalizations of Schubert polynomials, arising from $K$-theory of flag varieties rather than cohomology. We follow the presentation of \cite{GX2025}.

\begin{Definition}[Grothendieck polynomial]
\label{def:GrothendieckPolynomial}
The Grothendieck polynomial $\mathfrak{G}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \in \mathbb{Z}[\beta][x_1, \ldots, x_n; y_1, \ldots, y_n]$ for $w \in S_n$ is defined recursively by the \emph{divided difference operators} $\pi_i$ acting on the polynomial ring:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For the longest element $w_0 \in S_n$:
\[
\mathfrak{G}_{w_0}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) = \prod_{i+j \leq n} (x_i - y_j + \beta y_j);
\]

\item[(ii)] For any $w \in S_n$ and simple reflection $s_i$:
\[
\mathfrak{G}_{ws_i}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) = \mathfrak{G}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \frac{x_i - y_{w(i)} + \beta y_{w(i)}}{x_i - x_{w(i)}} - \beta \cdot \pi_i(\mathfrak{G}_w),
\]
where $\pi_i$ acts by
\[
\pi_i(f) = \frac{f - s_i(f)}{x_i - x_{w(i)}},
\]
and $s_i$ swaps $x_i$ and $x_{w(i)}$ while leaving other variables fixed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{Definition}

The parameter $\beta$ is a formal deformation parameter; setting $\beta = 0$ recovers the usual (double) Schubert polynomial:
\[
\mathfrak{G}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})\big|_{\beta=0} = \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}).
\]

The operator $\ominus$ appearing in the positivity conjecture is defined as:
\[
a \ominus b := \frac{a - b}{1 + \beta b} = \frac{a}{1 + \beta b} - \frac{b}{1 + \beta b}.
\]
When $\beta = 0$, this reduces to ordinary subtraction $a - b$.

\begin{Lemma}[Stability]
\label{Lem:GrothendieckStability}
The Grothendieck polynomials form a stable family: for $w \in S_n \subset S_{n+1}$, we have
\[
\mathfrak{G}_w^{(n+1)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) = \mathfrak{G}_w^{(n)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}),
\]
where the superscript indicates the ambient symmetric group.
\end{Lemma}

This stability allows us to define $\mathfrak{G}_w$ for all $w \in S_\infty$ by stabilization.

\section{Grothendieck Positivity Conjecture}

Building on the positivity phenomenon for Schubert polynomials, Gao--Xiong propose an analogous conjecture for Grothendieck polynomials.

\begin{Conjecture}[Grothendieck Positivity, {\cite[Conjecture 2.7]{GX2025}}]
\label{conj:GrothendieckPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_\infty$, consider the expansion of Grothendieck polynomials:
\[
\mathfrak{G}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{G}_v(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t}) = \sum_{w \in S_\infty} \tilde{c}_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \cdot \mathfrak{G}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t}).
\]
Then $\tilde{c}_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \in \mathbb{N}[\beta][t_i \ominus y_j]_{i,j \geq 1}$, where $a \ominus b := \frac{a - b}{1 + \beta b}$.
\end{Conjecture}

\begin{Remark}
The parameter $\beta$ is a formal variable that deformation-quantizes the difference $t_i - y_j$; when $\beta = 0$, the operator $\ominus$ reduces to ordinary subtraction. Proving \cref{conj:GrothendieckPositivity} would extend the Graham positivity phenomenon to the $K$-theoretic setting, where Grothendieck polynomials play the role of Schubert polynomials.
\end{Remark}

\begin{Conjecture}[Quantum Double Schubert Positivity]
\label{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_{\infty}$, the product of quantum double Schubert polynomials expands as:
\[
\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}; q) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_w^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})
\]
with coefficients satisfying:
\[
c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z}; q) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k]_{i,j \geq 1, k \geq 1}
\]
namely, polynomials in $y_i - z_j$ and quantum parameters $q_k$ with nonnegative integer coefficients.
\end{Conjecture}

\section{Geometric Interpretation}

The triple variables $(y_i - z_j, q_k)$ arise from three sources:

\begin{itemize}
\item $y_i$: records displacement from the first Schubert variety
\item $z_j$: records displacement from the second Schubert variety
\item $q_k$: records quantum corrections from curve counting (Gromov-Witten invariants)
\end{itemize}

The coefficient $c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y};\mathbf{z};q)$ can be interpreted as counting curves meeting three Schubert varieties in the quantum setting.

\section{Current State}

This conjecture remains open. Progress includes:

\begin{itemize}
\item Mihalcea's proof of positivity for two Schubert classes in equivariant quantum cohomology \cite{Mihalcea}
\item Gao-Xiong's work establishing the framework for triple Schubert calculus \cite{GX2025}
\item The full quantum double Schubert positivity conjecture is a frontier topic in algebraic geometry and representation theory
\end{itemize}

\section{Remarks}

The purpose of this chapter is to record this conjecture for future reference. Proving this conjecture would unify the positivity phenomena in both classical and quantum Schubert calculus.
