\section{Overview of Positivity Conjectures}
\label{sec:PositivityConjectureHierarchy}

In the preceding chapters we have progressively built up the positivity phenomenon in Schubert calculus. Each generalization introduced new variables or structures while preserving the core property of positivity. This chapter aims to survey the full picture of this conjecture hierarchy, clarify the relationships among the various conjectures, and introduce the currently unresolved open problem: the quantum double Schubert polynomial positivity conjecture.

\subsection{From Classical to Quantum Positivity}
\label{sec:ClassicalToQuantumPositivity}

The positivity phenomenon in Schubert calculus follows this evolutionary path:

\begin{sloppypar}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textbf{Classical Schubert Positivity}: in ordinary cohomology, the Littlewood--Richardson coefficients are nonnegative integers;
\item[(ii)] \textbf{Graham Positivity}: in equivariant cohomology, the structure constants upgrade to polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients in the negative root variables $x_i$;
\item[(iii)] \textbf{Mihalcea Positivity (Equivariant Quantum Positivity)}: in equivariant quantum cohomology, the quantum correction $q$ is added and the structure constants remain nonnegative polynomials;
\item[(iv)] \textbf{Double Schubert Positivity}: a second set of variables $\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}$ is introduced, and positivity is studied in the two-variable setting;
\item[(v)] \textbf{Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture}: simultaneously introduces both the two variables and the quantum correction --- this is the central open problem of the present chapter.
\end{enumerate}
\end{sloppypar}

Each level of positivity contains the previous level as a special case (by taking appropriate degeneration parameters). This "positivity stability" is not obvious and reflects the deep geometric structure underlying Schubert calculus.

\subsection{Core Statements of Each Level}
\label{sec:CoreStatementsEachLevel}

This subsection gives the precise statement of each level of the positivity conjecture.

\subsubsection{Classical Schubert Positivity}
\label{sec:ClassicalSchubertPositivityDetail}

In ordinary cohomology $H^*(G/B)$, the Littlewood--Richardson coefficients are intersection numbers, which are naturally nonnegative.\footnote{On the relationship between $\sigma^T(u)$ and $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x})$: see appendix \cref{sec:qa-sigma-vs-Schubert-polynomial}.}

\begin{Theorem}[Classical Schubert Positivity]
\label{th:ClassicalPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_n$, Schubert polynomials satisfy
$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^w \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x})$$
where the structure constant $c_{u,v}^w \in \mathbb{N}$ is a nonnegative integer, equal to the number of intersection points of three Schubert varieties in the flag manifold.
\end{Theorem}

Geometric meaning: $c_{u,v}^w$ counts point intersections of three Schubert varieties --- the most direct source of positivity.\footnote{问：什么是 Schubert variety？Schubert 多项式与 Schubert variety 的关系是什么？见附录 \cref{sec:qa-schubert-variety}。}

\subsubsection{Graham Positivity}
\label{sec:GrahamPositivityDetail}

In equivariant cohomology $H_T^*(G/B)$, the torus action parameters $x_i$ (corresponding to negative roots $-\alpha_i$) are introduced, and the structure constants upgrade to polynomials.

\begin{Theorem}[Graham Positivity Theorem {\cite[ Theorem 3.2]{Gr}}]
\label{th:GrahamPositivityOriginal}
Let $X = G/B$ be the flag manifold. Then
\[
\sigma^T(u) \cdot \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{w \in W} c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)
\]\footnote{问：$\sigma^T(u)$ 与 $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x})$ 的精确关系是什么？见附录 \cref{sec:qa-sigma-vs-Schubert-polynomial}。}
where $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients in the negative roots $-\alpha_i$.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Why do the $x_i$ appear?}\ $x_i$ come from the torus $T$ action parameters. In equivariant cohomology, the Schubert class is no longer the direct class of a subvariety but is lifted to a class on the homotopy quotient $X \times_T ET$. This lift turns intersection numbers into cohomology classes, and the structure of torus fixed points produces polynomial factors in $x_i$.

\textbf{Geometric intuition:} The key insight of Graham's proof is that even in the more abstract equivariant setting, the intersection of Schubert cycles retains a positivity structure. This positivity originates from moment map geometry, concretely realized through Gelfand--Tsetlin patterns (see \cite[Section 3]{Gr}).

\subsubsection{Mihalcea Positivity (Equivariant Quantum Cohomology)}
\label{sec:MihalceaPositivityDetail}

In equivariant quantum cohomology $QH_T^*(G/B)$, both the quantum correction $q$ and equivariant parameters $x_i$ are introduced simultaneously.

\begin{Theorem}[Mihalcea Positivity {\cite[ Theorem 1]{Mihalcea}}]
\label{th:MihalceaPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in W$ and curve degree $d$, the equivariant quantum product satisfies
$$\sigma^T(u) \star \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{d} \sum_{w \in W} q^d \, c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)$$
where $c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Geometric intuition:} Mihalcea's key insight is that via the projection formula, the curve-counting problem in quantum cohomology can be reduced to Graham positivity on $X \times X$. This reduction strategy will be discussed in detail in \S\ref{sec:TechniquesOverview}.

\textbf{Relationship with Graham Positivity:} When $d = 0$ (no quantum correction), Mihalcea positivity degenerates to Graham positivity:
$$c_{u,v}^{w,0}(\mathbf{x}) = c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_i].$$

\subsubsection{Double Graham Positivity (Samuel Conjecture)}
\label{sec:DoubleGrahamPositivityDetail}

In the framework of double Schubert polynomials $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$, Gao and Xiong proved positivity in a more general setting.

\begin{Theorem}[Triple Schubert Positivity / Samuel Conjecture {\cite[ Theorem 1.1]{GX2025}}]
\label{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}
Let $u, v \in S_\infty$ satisfy the separated descents condition (i.e., $\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$). Then
$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t}) = \sum_w c^w_{u,v}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t})$$
where $c^w_{u,v}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \in \mathbb{N}[t_i - y_j]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients in $t_i - y_j$.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Relationship with Graham Positivity:} When $u, v$ are both Grassmannian permutations, the separated descents condition is automatically satisfied, and \cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity} degenerates to Graham positivity.

\textbf{Relationship with the Kirillov Conjecture:} When $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$, the coefficients degenerate to nonnegative integers, which corresponds to the Kirillov conjecture (\cref{cor:Kirillov}): when the skew divided difference operator $\partial_{w/v}$ acts on Schubert polynomials, the coefficients are nonnegative.

\textbf{Relationship with Mihalcea Positivity:} Mihalcea studies positivity in equivariant quantum cohomology (involving the quantum correction $q$), while Gao and Xiong study positivity under double variables (without quantum correction). These are two independent directions of generalization.

\subsubsection{Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture (Open Problem)}
\label{sec:QuantumDoubleConjectureDetail}

Simultaneously introducing the two variables $(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z})$ and the quantum correction $q$, we obtain the most general positivity conjecture.

\begin{Conjecture}[Quantum Double Schubert Positivity]
\label{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_\infty$, quantum double Schubert polynomials satisfy
$$\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}; q) \, \mathfrak{S}_w^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})$$
where the coefficients satisfy
$$c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}; q) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k].$$
That is, the expansion coefficients are polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients in the differences $y_i - z_j$ and quantum variables $q_k$.
\end{Conjecture}

\textbf{On the naming:} The term "Quantum Double" carries two layers of meaning here:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Double}: each factor $\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$ is a double Schubert polynomial, with $\mathbf{y}$ recording relative displacement;
\item \textbf{Quantum}: the multiplication involves the quantum correction $q_k$ coming from curve counting.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Current status:} \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} is currently an open problem with no complete proof. It unifies two major known results: double Graham positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}) and Mihalcea positivity (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity}).

\section{Techniques of Proof}
\label{sec:TechniquesOverview}

This section overviews the key techniques used in the proofs of the various positivity theorems. These have been fully developed in Ch1 and Ch4; here we only review the ideas and intuitions behind the key lemmas.

\subsection{Ch1 Proof Techniques: Kirillov Positivity and Triple Schubert Positivity}
\label{sec:Ch1ProofTechniques}

Ch1 proved positivity in the double-variable setting (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}), relying on the following key lemmas.

\subsubsection{Lemma \cref{def:Lemma22} (Normal Subgroup Lemma)}
\label{sec:Lemma22Detail} (Normal Subgroup Lemma)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 2.2]{GX2025}): If $ws_i > w$, then $N^-(ws_i)$ is a normal subgroup of $N^-(w)$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Through analysis at the Lie algebra level --- $\mathfrak{n}^-(ws_i)$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak{n}^-(w)$. Using Humphreys \cite[Theorems 13.1, 13.3]{humphreys1975}, one derives the corresponding algebraic group-level normal subgroup relation from the Lie algebra-level ideal relation. This lemma is the cornerstone of the entire inductive construction --- it allows one to build up long reduced expressions from shorter ones.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} Establishes ideal relations among $B^-$-orbits, providing the structural foundation for the subsequent cycle decomposition (\cref{def:Corollary24}).\ 

\subsubsection{Lemma \cref{def:Lemma25} (Transverse Intersection Lemma)}
\label{sec:Lemma25Detail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 2.5]{GX2025}): Under the separated descents condition, $\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}$ is proper and transverse.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Using the product construction $w \times \pi \in S_{2n}$, the problem is reduced to the Kleiman--Bertini theorem on flag manifolds (see \cite[Section 19.3]{AndersonFulton}).\footnote{The Kleiman--Bertini theorem states that if $X$ is a smooth variety with a group $G$ acting transitively, and $Z_1, Z_2$ are regularly embedded subvarieties, then for generic $g \in G$ the intersection $gZ_1 \cap Z_2$ is either empty or smooth of expected dimension. This guarantees transversality and hence well-defined intersection numbers. See \cite[Chapter 19]{AndersonFulton}.} The separated descents condition guarantees transversality at generic points.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the key source of positivity --- transversality ensures that the intersection is "well-defined": no tangency, degeneration, or ambiguous point counts. The point count of a transverse intersection is a well-defined nonnegative integer, which produces nonnegative expansion coefficients.\ 

\textbf{Why is transversality so important?} Non-transverse intersections can have infinitely many points (two tangent curves) or an ambiguous count. The transversality condition turns geometric intersection into a topological transverse intersection, ensuring nonnegativity.\ 

\subsubsection{Corollary \cref{def:Corollary24} (Cycle Decomposition)}
\label{sec:Corollary24Detail} (Cycle Decomposition)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Corollary 2.4]{GX2025}): On the flag manifold $G/B$, any $B^-$-invariant effective cycle is a nonnegative linear combination of Schubert classes $[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Since the flag manifold has only finitely many $B^-$-orbits, any $B^-$-invariant effective cycle must be a linear combination of the Schubert cycles. Nonnegativity comes from geometric intuition --- each component of the cycle corresponds to the closure of some Schubert cell.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the bridge connecting geometric cycles to algebraic expansion coefficients --- it tells us: geometric cycle = nonnegative linear combination of Schubert classes, therefore expansion coefficients are nonnegative.\

\subsubsection{Tool: Theorem \ref{def:Theorem1064} (Schubert Class = Schubert Polynomial)}
\label{sec:Theorem1064Detail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[Theorem 10.6.4]{anderson2023}): We have $\sigma_w = \mathfrak{S}_w(x;y)$ in $H_T^* Fl(V)$.

\textbf{Variables:} $x_i = -c_1^T(\mathbb{S}_i/\mathbb{S}_{i-1})$, $y_i = -c_1^T(E^{i-1}/E^i)$.

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This theorem bridges geometry and algebra: it tells us that the geometric Schubert class $\sigma_w$ equals the algebraic Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_w(x;y)$. Consequently, the set $\{\mathfrak{S}_w \mid w \in S_n\}$ forms a $\mathbb{Z}[y]$-basis for $\mathbb{Z}[x,y]$. This basis property is what allows us to expand any product of Schubert classes in terms of the Schubert basis, and to study the positivity of coefficients through the polynomial structure. In Ch1, it appears in the proof strategy at the step where we identify $[\overline{B^-\pi B/B}]_T$ with the double Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_\pi(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{t})$.

\textbf{Proof technique:} The proof uses divided difference operators $\partial_k$ acting on the polynomial ring $\Lambda[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I$, combined with the geometry of $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundles over flag manifolds. Specifically, Lemma 10.6.5 shows that the pushforward map $pr_{2*}pr_1^*: H_T^* Fl \to H_T^* Fl$ corresponds to the divided difference operator $\partial_k$, which allows induction on the length of permutations. This algebraic approach is developed in \cite[Chapter 10]{anderson2023}.

\textbf{Relationship to other tools:} While Lemma \ref{def:Lemma25} guarantees geometric transversality (yielding nonnegative integer counts) and Corollary \ref{def:Corollary24} guarantees Schubert basis expansion (yielding nonnegative linear combinations), Theorem \ref{def:Theorem1064} guarantees polynomial representatives (enabling algebraic manipulation and explicit positivity computation). These three results form a complete toolkit: geometry $\to$ cohomology class $\to$ polynomial representative.

The detailed statement and proof are in \cite[Section 10.6, Theorem 10.6.4]{anderson2023}.

\subsection{Ch4 Proof Techniques: Mihalcea Equivariant Quantum Cohomology}
