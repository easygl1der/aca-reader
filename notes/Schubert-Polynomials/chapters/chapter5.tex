\chapter{Quantum Double Schubert Positivity Conjecture}
\label{chap:QuantumDoublePositivityConjecture}

\section{Overview of Positivity Conjectures}
\label{sec:PositivityConjectureHierarchy}

In the preceding chapters we have progressively built up the positivity phenomenon in Schubert calculus. Each generalization introduced new variables or structures while preserving the core property of positivity. This chapter aims to survey the full picture of this conjecture hierarchy, clarify the relationships among the various conjectures, and introduce the currently unresolved open problem: the quantum double Schubert polynomial positivity conjecture.

\subsection{From Classical to Quantum Positivity}

The positivity phenomenon in Schubert calculus follows this evolutionary path:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textbf{Classical Schubert Positivity}: in ordinary cohomology, the Littlewood--Richardson coefficients are nonnegative integers;
\item[(ii)] \textbf{Graham Positivity}: in equivariant cohomology, the structure constants upgrade to polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients in the negative root variables $x_i$;
\item[(iii)] \textbf{Mihalcea Positivity (Equivariant Quantum Positivity)}: in equivariant quantum cohomology, the quantum correction $q$ is added and the structure constants remain nonnegative polynomials;
\item[(iv)] \textbf{Double Schubert Positivity}: a second set of variables $\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}$ is introduced, and positivity is studied in the two-variable setting;
\item[(v)] \textbf{Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture}: simultaneously introduces both the two variables and the quantum correction --- this is the central open problem of the present chapter.
\end{enumerate}

Each level of positivity contains the previous level as a special case (by taking appropriate degeneration parameters). This "positivity stability" is not obvious and reflects the deep geometric structure underlying Schubert calculus.

\subsection{The Positivity Conjecture Hierarchy}

The following table compares the positivity conjectures at each level, from classical to quantum double:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
\hline
\textbf{Positivity Level} & \textbf{Polynomial Variables} & \textbf{Polynomial Ring} & \textbf{Status} \\ \hline
Classical Schubert Positivity & $\mathbf{x}$ & $\mathbb{N}$ (nonnegative integers) & Proved \\ \hline
Graham Positivity & $\mathbf{x}$ & $\mathbb{N}[x_i]$ (nonnegative polynomials) & Proved \\ \hline
Mihalcea Positivity & $\mathbf{x}, q$ & $\mathbb{N}[x_i][q]$ & Proved \\ \hline
Double Graham Positivity & $\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}$ & $\mathbb{N}[t_i - y_j]$ & Proved (\cite{GX2025})\\ \hline
Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture & $\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}, q$ & $\mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k]$ & \textbf{Open} \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Core Statements of Each Level}

This subsection gives the precise statement of each level of the positivity conjecture.

\subsubsection{Classical Schubert Positivity}

In ordinary cohomology $H^*(G/B)$, the Littlewood--Richardson coefficients are intersection numbers, which are naturally nonnegative.

\begin{Theorem}[Classical Schubert Positivity]
\label{th:ClassicalPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_n$, Schubert polynomials satisfy
$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^w \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x})$$
where the structure constant $c_{u,v}^w \in \mathbb{N}$ is a nonnegative integer, equal to the number of intersection points of three Schubert varieties in the flag manifold.
\end{Theorem}

Geometric meaning: $c_{u,v}^w$ counts point intersections of three Schubert varieties --- the most direct source of positivity.

\subsubsection{Graham Positivity}

In equivariant cohomology $H_T^*(G/B)$, the torus action parameters $x_i$ (corresponding to negative roots $-\alpha_i$) are introduced, and the structure constants upgrade to polynomials.

\begin{Theorem}[Graham Positivity Theorem {\cite[ Theorem 3.2]{Gr}}]
\label{th:GrahamPositivityOriginal}
Let $X = G/B$ be the flag manifold. Then
$$\sigma^T(u) \cdot \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{w \in W} c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)$$
where $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients in the negative roots $-\alpha_i$.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Why do the $x_i$ appear?}\ $x_i$ come from the torus $T$ action parameters. In equivariant cohomology, the Schubert class is no longer the direct class of a subvariety but is lifted to a class on the homotopy quotient $X \times_T ET$. This lift turns intersection numbers into cohomology classes, and the structure of torus fixed points produces polynomial factors in $x_i$.

\textbf{Geometric intuition:} The key insight of Graham's proof is that even in the more abstract equivariant setting, the intersection of Schubert cycles retains a positivity structure. This positivity originates from moment map geometry, concretely realized through Gelfand--Tsetlin patterns (see \cite[Section 3]{Gr}).

\subsubsection{Mihalcea Positivity (Equivariant Quantum Cohomology)}

In equivariant quantum cohomology $QH_T^*(G/B)$, both the quantum correction $q$ and equivariant parameters $x_i$ are introduced simultaneously.

\begin{Theorem}[Mihalcea Positivity {\cite[ Theorem 1]{Mihalcea}}]
\label{th:MihalceaPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in W$ and curve degree $d$, the equivariant quantum product satisfies
$$\sigma^T(u) \star \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{d} \sum_{w \in W} q^d \, c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)$$
where $c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Geometric intuition:} Mihalcea's key insight is that via the projection formula, the curve-counting problem in quantum cohomology can be reduced to Graham positivity on $X \times X$. This reduction strategy will be discussed in detail in \S\ref{sec:TechniquesOverview}.

\textbf{Relationship with Graham Positivity:} When $d = 0$ (no quantum correction), Mihalcea positivity degenerates to Graham positivity:
$$c_{u,v}^{w,0}(\mathbf{x}) = c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_i].$$

\subsubsection{Double Graham Positivity (Samuel Conjecture)}

In the framework of double Schubert polynomials $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$, Gao and Xiong proved positivity in a more general setting.

\begin{Theorem}[Triple Schubert Positivity / Samuel Conjecture {\cite[ Theorem 1.1]{GX2025}}]
\label{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}
Let $u, v \in S_\infty$ satisfy the separated descents condition (i.e., $\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$). Then
$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t}) = \sum_w c^w_{u,v}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t})$$
where $c^w_{u,v}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \in \mathbb{N}[t_i - y_j]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients in $t_i - y_j$.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Relationship with Graham Positivity:} When $u, v$ are both Grassmannian permutations, the separated descents condition is automatically satisfied, and \cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity} degenerates to Graham positivity.

\textbf{Relationship with the Kirillov Conjecture:} When $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$, the coefficients degenerate to nonnegative integers, which corresponds to the Kirillov conjecture (\cref{cor:Kirillov}): when the skew divided difference operator $\partial_{w/v}$ acts on Schubert polynomials, the coefficients are nonnegative.

\textbf{Relationship with Mihalcea Positivity:} Mihalcea studies positivity in equivariant quantum cohomology (involving the quantum correction $q$), while Gao and Xiong study positivity under double variables (without quantum correction). These are two independent directions of generalization.

\subsubsection{Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture (Open Problem)}

Simultaneously introducing the two variables $(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z})$ and the quantum correction $q$, we obtain the most general positivity conjecture.

\begin{Conjecture}[Quantum Double Schubert Positivity]
\label{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_\infty$, quantum double Schubert polynomials satisfy
$$\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}; q) \, \mathfrak{S}_w^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})$$
where the coefficients satisfy
$$c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}; q) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k].$$
That is, the expansion coefficients are polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients in the differences $y_i - z_j$ and quantum variables $q_k$.
\end{Conjecture}

\textbf{On the naming:} The term "Quantum Double" carries two layers of meaning here:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Double}: each factor $\mathfrak{S}_u^{(q)}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$ is a double Schubert polynomial, with $\mathbf{y}$ recording relative displacement;
\item \textbf{Quantum}: the multiplication involves the quantum correction $q_k$ coming from curve counting.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Current status:} \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} is currently an open problem with no complete proof. It unifies two major known results: double Graham positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}) and Mihalcea positivity (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity}).

\section{Techniques of Proof}
\label{sec:TechniquesOverview}

This section overviews the key techniques used in the proofs of the various positivity theorems. These have been fully developed in Ch1 and Ch4; here we only review the ideas and intuitions behind the key lemmas.

\subsection{Ch1 Proof Techniques: Kirillov Positivity and Triple Schubert Positivity}

Ch1 proved positivity in the double-variable setting (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}), relying on the following key lemmas.

\subsubsection{Lemma \ref{def:Lemma22} (Normal Subgroup Lemma)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 2.2]{GX2025}): If $ws_i > w$, then $N^-(ws_i)$ is a normal subgroup of $N^-(w)$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Through analysis at the Lie algebra level --- $\mathfrak{n}^-(ws_i)$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak{n}^-(w)$. Using Humphreys \cite[Theorems 13.1, 13.3]{humphreys1975}, one derives the corresponding algebraic group-level normal subgroup relation from the Lie algebra-level ideal relation. This lemma is the cornerstone of the entire inductive construction --- it allows one to build up long reduced expressions from shorter ones.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} Establishes ideal relations among $B^-$-orbits, providing the structural foundation for the subsequent cycle decomposition (\cref{def:Corollary24}).\ 

\subsubsection{Lemma \ref{def:Lemma25} (Transverse Intersection Lemma)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 2.5]{GX2025}): Under the separated descents condition, $\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}$ is proper and transverse.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Using the product construction $w \times \pi \in S_{2n}$, the problem is reduced to the Kleiman--Bertini theorem on flag manifolds (see \cite[Section 19.3]{AndersonFulton}). The separated descents condition guarantees transversality at generic points.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the key source of positivity --- transversality ensures that the intersection is "well-defined": no tangency, degeneration, or ambiguous point counts. The point count of a transverse intersection is a well-defined nonnegative integer, which produces nonnegative expansion coefficients.\ 

\textbf{Why is transversality so important?} Non-transverse intersections can have infinitely many points (two tangent curves) or an ambiguous count. The transversality condition turns geometric intersection into a topological transverse intersection, ensuring nonnegativity.\ 

\subsubsection{Corollary \ref{def:Corollary24} (Cycle Decomposition)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Corollary 2.4]{GX2025}): On the flag manifold $G/B$, any $B^-$-invariant effective cycle is a nonnegative linear combination of Schubert classes $[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Since the flag manifold has only finitely many $B^-$-orbits, any $B^-$-invariant effective cycle must be a linear combination of the Schubert cycles. Nonnegativity comes from geometric intuition --- each component of the cycle corresponds to the closure of some Schubert cell.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the bridge connecting geometric cycles to algebraic expansion coefficients --- it tells us: geometric cycle = nonnegative linear combination of Schubert classes, therefore expansion coefficients are nonnegative.\ 

\subsubsection{Summary of Relationships}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c}
\hline
\textbf{Lemma} & \textbf{Core Content} & \textbf{Source of Positivity} \\ \hline
Lemma \ref{def:Lemma22} & $N^-(ws_i) \trianglelefteq N^-(w)$ & Foundation of inductive construction \\ \hline
Lemma \ref{def:Lemma25} & Transverse intersection & Well-defined intersection count \\ \hline
Corollary \ref{def:Corollary24} & Cycle decomposition into Schubert basis & Nonnegative expansion coefficients \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Overall strategy:} Geometric intersection (guaranteed well-defined by Lemma \ref{def:Lemma25}) \(\xrightarrow{\text{Equivariant cohomology}}\) cycle class \(\xrightarrow{\text{Corollary \ref{def:Corollary24}}}\) nonnegative linear combination of Schubert classes \(\xrightarrow{\text{Coefficient comparison}}\) positivity conclusion.

The detailed derivation is in Ch1 \S\ref{sec:ProofMain} (\cref{chap:TriplePositivity}).

\subsection{Ch4 Proof Techniques: Mihalcea Equivariant Quantum Cohomology}

Ch4 proved positivity in the quantum setting (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity}), relying on the following key lemmas.

\subsubsection{Lemma \ref{prop:Lemma61F} (Construction of the Map $F$)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 6.1]{Mihalcea}): Define $F = (ev_1, ev_2) : \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X, d) \to X \times X$, which is a $T$-equivariant and proper map.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} $ev_1, ev_2$ are the evaluation maps at the first and second marked points, respectively. Their combination $(ev_1, ev_2)$ projects the moduli space of stable maps $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d)$ to $X \times X$. $T$-equivariance comes from the natural torus action on stable maps.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the key step that transfers the problem from the moduli space $\overline{\mathcal{M}}$ to $X \times X$ --- through this map we can exploit the known positivity on $X \times X$.\ 

\subsubsection{Lemma \ref{prop:ev3inverse} (Irreducible Components of $ev_3^{-1}(Y(w))$)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 6.4]{Mihalcea}): $ev_3^{-1}(Y(w))$ is $T$-stable and reduced, and its irreducible components have codimension $l(w)$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} $Y(w)$ is the Schubert variety closure in the flag manifold. The evaluation condition $ev_3(p_3) \in Y(w)$ for stable maps produces a specific subvariety in the moduli space. The key is understanding the geometry of this subvariety --- it consists of a disjoint union of irreducible components.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This lemma decomposes the complicated geometric object in the moduli space into several "manageable" irreducible components. Each component is treated separately, then combined via the push-forward formula.\ 

\subsubsection{Lemma \ref{prop:KleimanTransversality} (Kleiman Transversality)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 6.3]{Mihalcea}): Let $Z$ be an irreducible $G$-variety and $F: Z \to X \times X$ a $G$-equivariant map. Then $F^{-1}(X(u) \times Y(v))$ is reduced and locally irreducible, with codimension $c(u) + l(v)$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} This is a version of the Kleiman--Bertini theorem --- generic position arguments for $G$-equivariant maps guarantee transversality of the preimage. The dimension formula follows from additivity of dimensions (fiber dimension $+$ base space dimension).\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} Ensures that before applying the projection formula, all relevant geometric objects are well-defined --- no singularities, no degenerations. This is a prerequisite for the transition from geometry to algebra.\ 

\subsubsection{Corollary \ref{cor:GrahamOnXX} (Graham Positivity on $X \times X$)}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Corollary 6.2]{Mihalcea}): Let $V \subset X \times X$ be a $T$-stable subvariety. Then
$$[V]_T = \sum_i f_i \, [Y(u_i) \times Y(v_i)]_T$$
where $f_i \in \Lambda = \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ are polynomials with nonnegative coefficients.

\textbf{Proof strategy:} This is a direct application of Graham's theorem to the product space $X \times X$ --- because Schubert cells on $X \times X$ are of the form $Y(u) \times Y(v)$, and Graham's theorem tells us that any $T$-stable cycle can be written as a nonnegative linear combination of Schubert cells.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the end point of the entire reduction strategy --- it tells us that all operations on $X \times X$ produce only linear combinations with nonnegative polynomial coefficients.\ 

\subsubsection{Reduction Flow Chart}

The overall logical chain of Mihalcea's proof is as follows:

\begin{align*}
c_{u,v}^{w,d} &= \pi_*^T\left((ev_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (ev_2^T)^*\sigma(v)^T \cdot (ev_3^T)^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T\right) \\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Projection formula}} [X(u) \times X(v)]_T \cdot F_*^T\left((ev_3^T)^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T\right) \\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Lemma \ref{prop:ev3inverse}}} \sum_i a_i \, [X(u) \times X(v)]_T \cdot [F(V_i)]_T \\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Corollary \ref{cor:GrahamOnXX}}} \sum_i a_i \sum_j f_j^i \, [Y(u_j) \times Y(v_j)]_T \\
&\xrightarrow{\text{Projection formula}} \sum_i a_i \sum_j f_j^i \cdot \delta_{u,u_j} \cdot \delta_{v,v_j}
\end{align*}

Here $a_i \in \mathbb{N}$ (map degree) and $f_j^i \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$ (nonnegative polynomials), finally yielding $c_{u,v}^{w,d} \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$.

The detailed derivation is in Ch4 \S\ref{sec:appendix-eqc-proof} (\cref{chap:EquivariantQuantumPositivity}).

\section{Quantum Double Schubert Positivity and Its Relations}
\label{sec:QuantumDoublePositivityAndRelations}

This section gives the precise statement of the quantum double positivity conjecture (\cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}), clarifies its degeneration relations with all previously proved positivity theorems, and discusses the current open problems.

\subsection{Degeneration Relations: How the Conjecture Contains Known Results}

The core value of \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} is that it simultaneously generalizes two major known positivity results.

\subsubsection{$q = 0$: Degeneration to Double Graham Positivity}

When $q = 0$ (i.e., ignoring the quantum correction), \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} degenerates to double Graham positivity:

$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}) \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})$$

where $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j]$.

This is exactly the case proved by Gao and Xiong's triple Schubert positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}).

\subsubsection{$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$: Degeneration to Mihalcea Positivity}

When $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, the two-variable set degenerates to a single variable set, and \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} becomes

$$\sigma^T(u) \star \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{d,w} q^d \, c_{u,v}^{w,d} \, \sigma^T(w)$$

where $c_{u,v}^{w,d} \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$.

This is exactly Mihalcea's equivariant quantum cohomology positivity (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity}).

\subsubsection{$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$ and $d = 0$: Degeneration to Graham Positivity}

Continuing the degeneration, take $d = 0$ (no quantum correction) to obtain

$$\sigma^T(u) \cdot \sigma^T(v) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)$$

where $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$ --- namely Graham positivity (\cref{th:GrahamPositivityOriginal}).

\subsubsection{$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, $d = 0$ and $x_i = 0$: Degeneration to Classical Positivity}

Finally, taking $x_i = 0$ (forgetting the torus action) gives

$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x})$$

where $c_{u,v}^w \in \mathbb{N}$ --- namely classical Schubert positivity (\cref{th:ClassicalPositivity}).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c}
\hline
\textbf{Degeneration Condition} & \textbf{Degenerates to} \\ \hline
$q = 0$ & Double Graham Positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}, Gao--Xiong)\\ \hline
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$ & Mihalcea Positivity (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity})\\ \hline
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, $d = 0$ & Graham Positivity (\cref{th:GrahamPositivityOriginal})\\ \hline
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, $d = 0$, $x_i = 0$ & Classical Schubert Positivity (\cref{th:ClassicalPositivity})\\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Remark:} In \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}, taking $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$ instead of $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$ yields the form of Mihalcea positivity. The two degeneration paths are mathematically equivalent.

\subsection{Relationship with the Kirillov Conjecture}

The Kirillov conjecture (\cref{cor:Kirillov}) asserts that when the skew divided difference operator $\partial_{w/v}$ acts on Schubert polynomials, the coefficients are nonnegative.

Taking $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$ and $d = 0$ in \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} gives Mihalcea positivity; taking the further limit $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$ and $x_i = 0$ degenerates to the classical case --- this is exactly the situation described by the Kirillov conjecture.

Thus \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} implies the Kirillov conjecture through the following chain:
$$\text{Quantum double positivity} \xrightarrow{q=0} \text{Double Graham positivity} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{t}=0} \text{Kirillov conjecture}.$$

\subsection{Open Problems}

Although we have established the positivity conjecture hierarchy (Classical $\to$ Graham $\to$ Mihalcea $\to$ Double Graham), the quantum double positivity conjecture (\cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}) remains open. Its main challenges are:

\textbf{Open Problem 1: Establishing the geometric framework.} In proving Mihalcea positivity, the projection formula reduces the curve-counting problem to Graham positivity on $X \times X$. In the double-variable framework, what are the corresponding geometric objects? Can the flag manifold geometry on $X \times X$ support the double-variable structure equally well?

\textbf{Open Problem 2: A quantum version of the separated descents condition.} In the proof of double Graham positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}), the separated descents condition ($\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$) is the key hypothesis guaranteeing transverse intersection. In the quantum double setting, does a similar weakened condition exist? Or must stronger restrictions on $u, v$ be imposed?

\textbf{Open Problem 3: Interaction between quantum parameters $q_k$ and variables $y_i - z_j$.} In Mihalcea's proof, the quantum correction $q^d$ and the equivariant polynomials $c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x})$ are separated. But in the double framework, the interaction between quantum parameters $q_k$ and $y_i - z_j$ may introduce new complexity --- the two may mix to produce novel monomial structures, thereby altering the positivity analysis.

\textbf{Open Problem 4: The case of general flag varieties $G/P$.} The above discussion mainly addresses the flag manifold $Fl_n = GL_n/B$. For a general partial flag variety $G/P$, does quantum double positivity still hold? This involves more complex algebraic geometric structures.

\textbf{Possible breakthrough direction:} A natural strategy is to attempt to extend Mihalcea's reduction method to the double framework. Concretely, we need to find:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$*$] a proper map $F: \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d) \to X \times X$;
\item[$*$] a double version of Graham positivity on $X \times X$ (which may require leveraging the equivariant cohomology geometry from \cite{AndersonFulton});
\item[$*$] a projection formula that reduces the coefficients of double equivariant quantum cohomology to the above double Graham positivity.
\end{enumerate}
This strategy is in the same spirit as the proof approaches in Ch1 and Ch4, but concrete implementation remains to be explored.
