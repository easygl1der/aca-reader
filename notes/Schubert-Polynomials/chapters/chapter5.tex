\section{Overview of Positivity Conjectures}
\label{sec:PositivityConjectureHierarchy}

In the preceding chapters we have progressively built up the positivity phenomenon in Schubert calculus. Each generalization introduced new variables or structures while preserving the core property of positivity. This chapter aims to survey the full picture of this conjecture hierarchy, clarify the relationships among the various conjectures, and introduce the currently unresolved open problem: the quantum double Schubert polynomial positivity conjecture.

\subsection{From Classical to Quantum Positivity}
\label{sec:ClassicalToQuantumPositivity}

The positivity phenomenon in Schubert calculus follows this evolutionary path:

\begin{sloppypar}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textbf{Classical Schubert Positivity}: in ordinary cohomology, the Littlewood--Richardson coefficients are nonnegative integers;
\item[(ii)] \textbf{Graham Positivity}: in equivariant cohomology, the structure constants upgrade to polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients in the negative root variables $x_i$;
\item[(iii)] \textbf{Mihalcea Positivity (Equivariant Quantum Positivity)}: in equivariant quantum cohomology, the quantum correction $q$ is added and the structure constants remain nonnegative polynomials;
\item[(iv)] \textbf{Double Schubert Positivity}: a second set of variables $\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}$ is introduced, and positivity is studied in the two-variable setting;
\item[(v)] \textbf{Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture}: simultaneously introduces both the two variables and the quantum correction --- this is the central open problem of the present chapter.
\end{enumerate}
\end{sloppypar}

Each level of positivity contains the previous level as a special case (by taking appropriate degeneration parameters). This "positivity stability" is not obvious and reflects the deep geometric structure underlying Schubert calculus.

\subsection{Core Statements of Each Level}
\label{sec:CoreStatementsEachLevel}

This subsection gives the precise statement of each level of the positivity conjecture.

\subsubsection{Classical Schubert Positivity}
\label{sec:ClassicalSchubertPositivityDetail}

In ordinary cohomology $H^*(G/B)$, the Littlewood--Richardson coefficients are intersection numbers, which are naturally nonnegative.\footnote{On the relationship between $\sigma^T(u)$ and $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x})$: see appendix \cref{sec:qa-sigma-vs-Schubert-polynomial}.}

\begin{Theorem}[Classical Schubert Positivity]
\label{th:ClassicalPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_n$, Schubert polynomials satisfy
$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^w \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x})$$
where the structure constant $c_{u,v}^w \in \mathbb{N}$ is a nonnegative integer, equal to the number of intersection points of three Schubert varieties in the flag manifold.
\end{Theorem}

Geometric meaning: $c_{u,v}^w$ counts point intersections of three Schubert varieties --- the most direct source of positivity.\footnote{问：什么是 Schubert variety？Schubert 多项式与 Schubert variety 的关系是什么？见附录 \cref{sec:qa-schubert-variety}。}

\subsubsection{Graham Positivity}
\label{sec:GrahamPositivityDetail}

In equivariant cohomology $H_T^*(G/B)$, the torus action parameters $x_i$ (corresponding to negative roots $-\alpha_i$) are introduced, and the structure constants upgrade to polynomials.

\begin{Theorem}[Graham Positivity Theorem {\cite[ Theorem 3.2]{Gr}}]
\label{th:GrahamPositivityOriginal}
Let $X = G/B$ be the flag manifold. Then
\[
\sigma^T(u) \cdot \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{w \in W} c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)
\]\footnote{问：$\sigma^T(u)$ 与 $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x})$ 的精确关系是什么？见附录 \cref{sec:qa-sigma-vs-Schubert-polynomial}。}
其中 $\sigma^T(w)$ 是 Schubert 类 $[\Omega_w]_T$ 的代数记号；在量子情形 $QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n)$ 中对应记为 $\sigma^T(w)$ 或 $[\Omega_w]_{U_n}$，两者的区别见附录 \cref{sec:qa-QuantumSchubertClassNotation}。
where $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients in the negative roots $-\alpha_i$.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Why do the $x_i$ appear?}\ $x_i$ come from the torus $T$ action parameters. In equivariant cohomology, the Schubert class is no longer the direct class of a subvariety but is lifted to a class on the homotopy quotient $X \times_T ET$. This lift turns intersection numbers into cohomology classes, and the structure of torus fixed points produces polynomial factors in $x_i$.

\textbf{Geometric intuition:} The key insight of Graham's proof is that even in the more abstract equivariant setting, the intersection of Schubert cycles retains a positivity structure. This positivity originates from moment map geometry, concretely realized through Gelfand--Tsetlin patterns (see \cite[Section 3]{Gr}).

\subsubsection{Mihalcea Positivity (Equivariant Quantum Cohomology)}
\label{sec:MihalceaPositivityDetail}

In equivariant quantum cohomology $QH_T^*(G/B)$, both the quantum correction $q$ and equivariant parameters $x_i$ are introduced simultaneously.

\begin{Theorem}[Mihalcea Positivity {\cite[ Theorem 1]{Mihalcea}}]
\label{th:MihalceaPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in W$ and curve degree $d$, the equivariant quantum product satisfies
$$\sigma^T(u) \star \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{d} \sum_{w \in W} q^d \, c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)$$\footnote{量子上同调乘积 $\star$ 的几何定义为 Gromov--Witten 理论中的稳定映射相交：$\sigma^T(u) \star \sigma^T(v) = \sum_d \mathrm{ev}_1^{-1}(\Omega_u) \cap \mathrm{ev}_2^{-1}(\Omega_v)$，这与普通 Schubert 簇在 $Fl_n$ 中相交的本质区别在于：量子情形计数的是 degree-$d$ 稳定映射 $\mathbb{P}^1 \to Fl_n$ 的个数，而非旗流形中点的个数。详见附录 \cref{sec:qa-QuantumStarProductGeometry}。}
where $c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Geometric intuition:} Mihalcea's key insight is that via the projection formula, the curve-counting problem in quantum cohomology can be reduced to Graham positivity on $X \times X$. This reduction strategy will be discussed in detail in \S\ref{sec:TechniquesOverview}.

\textbf{Relationship with Graham Positivity:} When $d = 0$ (no quantum correction), Mihalcea positivity degenerates to Graham positivity:
$$c_{u,v}^{w,0}(\mathbf{x}) = c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_i].$$

\subsubsection{Double Graham Positivity (Samuel Conjecture)}
\label{sec:DoubleGrahamPositivityDetail}

In the framework of double Schubert polynomials $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$, Gao and Xiong proved positivity in a more general setting.

\begin{Theorem}[Triple Schubert Positivity / Samuel Conjecture {\cite[ Theorem 1.1]{GX2025}}]
\label{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}
Let $u, v \in S_\infty$ satisfy the separated descents condition (i.e., $\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$). Then
$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t}) = \sum_w c^w_{u,v}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{t})$$
where $c^w_{u,v}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{t}) \in \mathbb{N}[t_i - y_j]$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients in $t_i - y_j$.
\end{Theorem}

\textbf{Relationship with Graham Positivity:} When $u, v$ are both Grassmannian permutations, the separated descents condition is automatically satisfied, and \cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity} degenerates to Graham positivity.

\textbf{Relationship with the Kirillov Conjecture:} When $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$, the coefficients degenerate to nonnegative integers, which corresponds to the Kirillov conjecture (\cref{cor:Kirillov}): when the skew divided difference operator $\partial_{w/v}$ acts on Schubert polynomials, the coefficients are nonnegative.

\textbf{Relationship with Mihalcea Positivity:} Mihalcea studies positivity in equivariant quantum cohomology (involving the quantum correction $q$), while Gao and Xiong study positivity under double variables (without quantum correction). These are two independent directions of generalization.

\subsubsection{Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture (Open Problem)}
\label{sec:QuantumDoubleConjectureDetail}

Simultaneously introducing the two variables $(\mathbf{y}; \mathbf{z})$ and the quantum correction $q$, we obtain the most general positivity conjecture.

\begin{Conjecture}[Quantum Double Schubert Positivity]
\label{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}
For $u, v, w \in S_\infty$, quantum double Schubert polynomials satisfy
$$\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{v}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}; q) \, \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{w}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})$$
where the coefficients satisfy
$$c_{u,v}^{w,(q)}(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}; q) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k].$$
That is, the expansion coefficients are polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients in the differences $y_i - z_j$ and quantum variables $q_k$.
\end{Conjecture}

\textbf{On the naming:} The term "Quantum Double" carries two layers of meaning here:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Double}: each factor $\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y})$ is a double Schubert polynomial, with $\mathbf{y}$ recording relative displacement;
\item \textbf{Quantum}: the multiplication involves the quantum correction $q_k$ coming from curve counting.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Current status:} \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} is currently an open problem with no complete proof. It unifies two major known results: double Graham positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}) and Mihalcea positivity (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity}).

\section{Techniques of Proof}
\label{sec:TechniquesOverview}

This section overviews the key techniques used in the proofs of the various positivity theorems. These have been fully developed in Ch1 and Ch4; here we only review the ideas and intuitions behind the key lemmas.

\paragraph*{The Foundational Bridge: Schubert Varieties, Schubert Classes, and Schubert Polynomials.}

Before listing the individual tools, we recall the fundamental identification that underlies the entire positivity story. The flag manifold $G/B$ admits a Bruhat decomposition
$$G/B = \bigsqcup_{w \in W} B^-wB/B,$$
so the Schubert varieties $\Omega_w = \overline{B^-wB/B}$ are the geometric building blocks. Their equivariant fundamental classes
$$\sigma_w = [\Omega_w]_T \in H_T^*(G/B)$$
form a $H_T^*$-basis of the equivariant cohomology ring. Via the Borel presentation $H_T^*(G/B) \cong \mathbb{Z}[x,y]/\mathcal{I}$ (\cite[Theorem 15.6.1]{AndersonFulton}), each Schubert class $\sigma_w$ is represented by a unique polynomial, the double Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_w(x;y)$ (\cite[Theorem 10.6.4]{AndersonFulton}):
$$\sigma_w = \mathfrak{S}_w(x;y) \quad \text{in } H_T^* Fl(V).$$
Consequently, the product of Schubert classes $\sigma_u \cdot \sigma_v = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w \sigma_w$ corresponds under this isomorphism to the product of Schubert polynomials $\mathfrak{S}_u \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w \mathfrak{S}_w$, with the same structure constants $c_{u,v}^w$. This explains why positivity at the geometric level (nonnegative intersection numbers) translates directly into positivity at the polynomial level (nonnegative coefficients in the expansion).

All tools listed in this section operate at one of these three levels:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geometric level} (Lemmas \ref{def:Lemma22}, \ref{def:Lemma25}): they ensure geometric objects (intersections of Schubert varieties) are well-defined and admit non-negative cycle decompositions.
    \item \textbf{Cohomological level} (Corollary \ref{def:Corollary24}): it guarantees that $T$-invariant or $B^-$-invariant cycles expand non-negatively in the Schubert basis.
    \item \textbf{Algebraic level} (Theorem \ref{def:Theorem1064}): it identifies the Schubert basis with Schubert polynomials, enabling explicit polynomial computation.
\end{itemize}
The tools from Ch1 work entirely within the double-variable setting $H_T^*(G/B)$, while the tools from Ch4 extend the story to the quantum setting $QH_T^*(G/B)$.


\subsection{Ch1 Proof Techniques: Kirillov Positivity and Triple Schubert Positivity}
\label{sec:Ch1ProofTechniques}

Ch1 proved positivity in the double-variable setting (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}), relying on the following key lemmas.

\subsubsection{Lemma \cref{def:Lemma22} (Normal Subgroup Lemma)}
\label{sec:Lemma22Detail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 2.2]{GX2025}): If $ws_i > w$, then $N^-(ws_i)$ is a normal subgroup of $N^-(w)$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Through analysis at the Lie algebra level --- $\mathfrak{n}^-(ws_i)$ is an ideal of $\mathfrak{n}^-(w)$. Using Humphreys \cite[Theorems 13.1, 13.3]{humphreys1975}, one derives the corresponding algebraic group-level normal subgroup relation from the Lie algebra-level ideal relation. This lemma is the cornerstone of the entire inductive construction --- it allows one to build up long reduced expressions from shorter ones.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} Establishes ideal relations among $B^-$-orbits, providing the structural foundation for the subsequent cycle decomposition (\cref{def:Corollary24}).\ 

\subsubsection{Lemma \cref{def:Lemma25} (Transverse Intersection Lemma)}
\label{sec:Lemma25Detail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 2.5]{GX2025}): Under the separated descents condition, $\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}$ is proper and transverse.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Using the product construction $w \times \pi \in S_{2n}$, the problem is reduced to the Kleiman--Bertini theorem on flag manifolds (see \cite[Section 19.3]{AndersonFulton}).\footnote{The Kleiman--Bertini theorem states that if $X$ is a smooth variety with a group $G$ acting transitively, and $Z_1, Z_2$ are regularly embedded subvarieties, then for generic $g \in G$ the intersection $gZ_1 \cap Z_2$ is either empty or smooth of expected dimension. This guarantees transversality and hence well-defined intersection numbers. See \cite[Chapter 19]{AndersonFulton}.} The separated descents condition guarantees transversality at generic points.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the key source of positivity --- transversality ensures that the intersection is "well-defined": no tangency, degeneration, or ambiguous point counts. The point count of a transverse intersection is a well-defined nonnegative integer, which produces nonnegative expansion coefficients.\ 

\textbf{Why is transversality so important?} Non-transverse intersections can have infinitely many points (two tangent curves) or an ambiguous count. The transversality condition turns geometric intersection into a topological transverse intersection, ensuring nonnegativity.\ 

\subsubsection{Corollary \cref{def:Corollary24} (Cycle Decomposition)}
\label{sec:Corollary24Detail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Corollary 2.4]{GX2025}): On the flag manifold $G/B$, any $B^-$-invariant effective cycle is a nonnegative linear combination of Schubert classes $[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} Since the flag manifold has only finitely many $B^-$-orbits, any $B^-$-invariant effective cycle must be a linear combination of the Schubert cycles. Nonnegativity comes from geometric intuition --- each component of the cycle corresponds to the closure of some Schubert cell.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the bridge connecting geometric cycles to algebraic expansion coefficients --- it tells us: geometric cycle = nonnegative linear combination of Schubert classes, therefore expansion coefficients are nonnegative.\

\subsubsection{Tool: Theorem \ref{def:Theorem1064} (Schubert Class = Schubert Polynomial)}
\label{sec:Theorem1064Detail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[Theorem 10.6.4]{AndersonFulton}): We have $\sigma_w = \mathfrak{S}_w(x;y)$ in $H_T^* Fl(V)$.

\textbf{Variables:} $x_i = -c_1^T(\mathbb{S}_i/\mathbb{S}_{i-1})$, $y_i = -c_1^T(E^{i-1}/E^i)$.

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This theorem bridges geometry and algebra: it tells us that the geometric Schubert class $\sigma_w$ equals the algebraic Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_w(x;y)$. Consequently, the set $\{\mathfrak{S}_w \mid w \in S_n\}$ forms a $\mathbb{Z}[y]$-basis for $\mathbb{Z}[x,y]$. This basis property is what allows us to expand any product of Schubert classes in terms of the Schubert basis, and to study the positivity of coefficients through the polynomial structure. In Ch1, it appears in the proof strategy at the step where we identify $[\overline{B^-\pi B/B}]_T$ with the double Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_\pi(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{t})$.

\textbf{Proof technique:} The proof uses divided difference operators $\partial_k$ acting on the polynomial ring $\Lambda[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I$, combined with the geometry of $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundles over flag manifolds. Specifically, Lemma 10.6.5 shows that the pushforward map $pr_{2*}pr_1^*: H_T^* Fl \to H_T^* Fl$ corresponds to the divided difference operator $\partial_k$, which allows induction on the length of permutations. This algebraic approach is developed in \cite[Chapter 10]{AndersonFulton}.

\textbf{Relationship to other tools:} While Lemma \ref{def:Lemma25} guarantees geometric transversality (yielding nonnegative integer counts) and Corollary \ref{def:Corollary24} guarantees Schubert basis expansion (yielding nonnegative linear combinations), Theorem \ref{def:Theorem1064} guarantees polynomial representatives (enabling algebraic manipulation and explicit positivity computation). These three results form a complete toolkit: geometry $\to$ cohomology class $\to$ polynomial representative.

The detailed statement and proof are in \cite[Section 10.6, Theorem 10.6.4]{AndersonFulton}.

\subsection{Ch4 Proof Techniques: Mihalcea Equivariant Quantum Cohomology}

\label{sec:Ch4ProofTechniques}

Ch4 proved positivity in the quantum setting (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity}), relying on the following key lemmas.

\subsubsection{Lemma \cref{prop:Lemma61F}}
\label{sec:Lemma61FDetail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 6.1]{Mihalcea}): Define $F = (ev_1, ev_2) : \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X, d) \to X \times X$, which is a $T$w-equivariant and proper map.\footnote{Notation: $X(u) = \overline{B^+uB/B}$ and $Y(v) = \overline{B^-vB/B}$ denote the (opposite) Schubert varieties in the flag manifold $G/B$, i.e. closures of the $B^+$- and $B^-$-orbits of the point $u \cdot B/B$. Their codimensions are $l(u)$ and $l(v)$ respectively. See \cite[Section 10.1]{FultonQuntum1997}.} 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} $ev_1, ev_2$ are the evaluation maps at the first and second marked points, respectively. Their combination $(ev_1, ev_2)$ projects the moduli space of stable maps $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d)$ to $X \times X$. $T$-equivariance comes from the natural torus action on stable maps.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the key step that transfers the problem from the moduli space $\overline{\mathcal{M}}$ to $X \times X$ --- through this map we can exploit the known positivity on $X \times X$.\ 

\subsubsection{Lemma \cref{prop:ev3inverse}}
\label{sec:ev3inverseDetail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 6.4]{Mihalcea}): $ev_3^{-1}(Y(w))$ is $T$-stable and reduced, and its irreducible components have codimension $l(w)$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} $Y(w)$ is the Schubert variety closure in the flag manifold. The evaluation condition $ev_3(p_3) \in Y(w)$ for stable maps produces a specific subvariety in the moduli space. The key is understanding the geometry of this subvariety --- it consists of a disjoint union of irreducible components.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This lemma decomposes the complicated geometric object in the moduli space into several "manageable" irreducible components. Each component is treated separately, then combined via the push-forward formula.\ 

\subsubsection{Lemma \cref{prop:KleimanTransversality}}
\label{sec:KleimanDetail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Lemma 6.3]{Mihalcea}): Let $Z$ be an irreducible $G$-variety and $F: Z \to X \times X$ a $G$-equivariant map. Then $F^{-1}(X(u) \times Y(v))$ is reduced and locally irreducible, with codimension $c(u) + l(v)$.\ 

\textbf{Proof strategy:} This is a version of the Kleiman--Bertini theorem --- generic position arguments for $G$-equivariant maps guarantee transversality of the preimage. The dimension formula follows from additivity of dimensions (fiber dimension $+$ base space dimension).\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} Ensures that before applying the projection formula, all relevant geometric objects are well-defined --- no singularities, no degenerations. This is a prerequisite for the transition from geometry to algebra.\ 

\subsubsection{Corollary \cref{cor:GrahamOnXX}}
\label{sec:GrahamOnXXDetail}

\textbf{Statement} (\cite[ Corollary 6.2]{Mihalcea}): Let $V \subset X \times X$ be a $T$-stable subvariety. Then
$$[V]_T = \sum_i f_i \, [Y(u_i) \times Y(v_i)]_T$$
where $f_i \in \Lambda = \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ are polynomials with nonnegative coefficients.

\textbf{Proof strategy:} This is a direct application of Graham's theorem to the product space $X \times X$ --- because Schubert cells on $X \times X$ are of the form $Y(u) \times Y(v)$, and Graham's theorem tells us that any $T$-stable cycle can be written as a nonnegative linear combination of Schubert cells.\ 

\textbf{Role in the positivity proof:} This is the end point of the entire reduction strategy --- it tells us that all operations on $X \times X$ produce only linear combinations with nonnegative polynomial coefficients.\ 

\subsubsection{Reduction Flow Chart}
\label{sec:ReductionFlowChart}

The overall logical chain of Mihalcea's proof is as follows (see \cref{eq:ReductionFlowChart}):\footnote{The derivation below follows \cite[Section 6]{Mihalcea} and is reproduced in full detail in Ch4 \S\cref{sec:appendix-eqc-proof} (\cref{chap:EquivariantQuantumPositivity}).}

\begin{align}
\label{eq:ReductionFlowChart}
c_{u,v}^{w,d} &= \pi_*^T(ev_1^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot ev_2^*\sigma(v)^T \cdot ev_3^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T) \tag{Step 1: definition} \\
&= (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T F_*^T\left((F^T)^*\!\big[(pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma(v)^T\big] \cdot (ev_3^T)^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T\right) \tag{Step 1$'$\,: rewrite} \\[4pt]
&\xrightarrow{\;\text{Projection formula}\;} (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T\!\Big(\big[(pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma(v)^T\big] \cdot F_*^T\big((ev_3^T)^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T\big)\Big) \tag{Step 2: apply $F_*^T$} \\[4pt]
&\xrightarrow{\;\text{Lemma }\cref{prop:ev3inverse}\;} \sum_i a_i \; (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T\!\Big((pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma(v)^T \cdot [F(V_i)]_T\Big) \tag{Step 3: irreducible components} \\[4pt]
&\xrightarrow{\;\text{Cor }\cref{cor:GrahamOnXX}\;} \sum_i a_i \sum_j f_j^i \; (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T\!\Big((pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma(v)^T \cdot [Y(u_j)\times Y(v_j)]_T\Big) \tag{Step 4: apply Graham on $X\times X$} \\[4pt]
&= \sum_i a_i \sum_j f_j^i \; \delta_{u,u_j} \cdot \delta_{v,v_j}. \tag{Step 5: equivariant Poincar\'e pairing}
\end{align}

Here $a_i \in \mathbb{N}$ (map degrees) and $f_j^i \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$ (nonnegative polynomials), finally yielding $c_{u,v}^{w,d} \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$. The equivalence between the original definition $c_{u,v}^{w,d} = \pi_*^T(ev_1^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot ev_2^*\sigma(v)^T \cdot ev_3^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T)$ and Step $1'$ follows from the identity $ev_1^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot ev_2^*\sigma(v)^T = (F^T)^*[(pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma(v)^T]$, where $F = (ev_1, ev_2): \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d) \to X \times X$ (see Ch4 \S\cref{sec:appendix-eqc-proof} (\cref{chap:EquivariantQuantumPositivity}, equation (1)), or equivalently the appendix: \S\cref{sec:qa-product-class-pullback}). Step 5 uses the equivariant Poincar\'e pairing $\langle\sigma^T(u),\widetilde{\sigma}^T(v)\rangle_T = \delta_{u,v}$ from \cite[Proposition 3.1]{Mihalcea}.

\subsubsection{Quantum Double Schubert Polynomials: Definition and Geometric Realization}
\label{sec:QuantumDoubleDefInCh5}

Before reviewing the lemmas, we record the precise definition of quantum double Schubert polynomials and their geometric realization, since this is the central algebraic object in Ch5.

\begin{Definition}[Quantum double Schubert polynomial {\cite[ Definition 4]{KM}}]
\label{def:QuantumDoubleSchubertCh5}
For each permutation $w \in S_n$, the \textbf{quantum double Schubert polynomial} is
\[
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \partial_{ww_0}^{(\mathbf{y})} \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{w_0}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}),
\]
where the top polynomial $\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{w_0}$ is
\[
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{w_0}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = \prod_{i=1}^{n-1} \Delta_i(y_{n-i} \mid x_1, \ldots, x_i),
\qquad
\Delta_k(t \mid x_1, \ldots, x_k) = \sum_{j=0}^k t^{k-j} e_j(x_1,\ldots,x_k \mid q_1,\ldots,q_{k-1}),
\]
with $e_j(\cdots \mid q)$ the quantum elementary symmetric functions.
\end{Definition}

\textbf{Geometric realization:} The quantum double Schubert polynomials represent Schubert classes in the \textbf{equivariant quantum cohomology ring} of the flag manifold $Fl_n = SL_n/B$:
\[
QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n) \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\ldots,x_n;\; y_1,\ldots,y_n;\; q_1,\ldots,q_{n-1}] / \widetilde{J},
\qquad
\widetilde{J} = \big\langle e_i(x \mid q_1,\ldots,q_{n-1}) - e_i(y_1,\ldots,y_n) \;\big|\; 1 \leq i \leq n \big\rangle,
\tag{\cite[ Theorem 8]{KM}}
\]\footnote{这里的 $U_n = U(n)$（酉群），下标 $U_n$ 表示关于 $U(n)$ 的极大 torus $T_{U_n} = (S^1)^n$ 等变。详见附录 \cref{sec:qa-U_n-QH}。}
where $\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})$ corresponds to the Schubert class $\sigma_w$ in this ring. The ideal $\widetilde{J}$ encodes the quantum correction: setting all $q_k = 0$ recovers the classical Borel ideal, and setting $y_i = 0$ recovers the single-variable quantum Schubert polynomials of \cite{FominGelfandPostnikov1997}.

\textbf{Classical degenerations:}
\[
\left. \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \right|_{q=0} = \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),
\qquad
\left. \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \right|_{y=0} = \mathfrak{S}_w^{\mathrm{quantum}}(\mathbf{x}),
\]
so both the classical double Schubert polynomial and the Fomin--Gelfand--Postnikov quantum Schubert polynomial appear as specializations.

\textbf{References:} Kirillov--Maeno \cite{KM} for the algebraic definition; Givental--Kim \cite{GiventalKim1995} and Ciocan-Fontanine \cite{CiocanFontanine1997} for the geometric realization of $QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n)$; Fomin--Gelfand--Postnikov \cite{FominGelfandPostnikov1997} for the alternative quantum Schubert polynomial construction.

\textbf{Variety--polynomial correspondence.} Under the Borel isomorphism $H_T^*(Fl_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}[x,y]/\mathcal{I}$, the classical double Schubert polynomial $\mathfrak{S}_w(x,y)$ corresponds to the Schubert class $[\Omega_w]_T$ of the Schubert variety $\Omega_w = \overline{B^-wB/B} \subset Fl_n$. In the quantum setting, the same correspondence holds after replacing $H_T^*$ by $QH_{U_n}^*$ and $\mathbb{Z}[x,y]/\mathcal{I}$ by $\mathbb{Z}[x,y,q]/\widetilde{J}$. Explicitly:
$$\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}) \;\xrightarrow{\;QH_{U_n}^*\text{ 同构}\;}\; [\Omega_w]_{U_n} \;\in\; QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n).$$\footnote{在 $QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n)$ 中，Schubert 类同时记为 $\sigma^T(w)$（代数记号）和 $[\Omega_w]_{U_n}$（几何记号），两者是同一个类在不同记号系统下的表示。详见附录 \cref{sec:qa-QuantumSchubertClassNotation}。}
In words: $\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w$ is the polynomial representative of the equivariant fundamental class of the Schubert variety $\Omega_w$ in the quantum setting. Consequently, the quantum double product $\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_u \star \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_v = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w(q) \, \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w$ mirrors the geometric quantum intersection product $[\Omega_u]_{U_n} \star [\Omega_v]_{U_n} = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w(q) \, [\Omega_w]_{U_n}$ on the flag manifold, with the same structure constants $c_{u,v}^w(q)$ encoding curve-counting Gromov--Witten invariants.

\section{Quantum Double Schubert Positivity and Its Relations}
\label{sec:QuantumDoublePositivityAndRelations}

This section gives the precise statement of the quantum double positivity conjecture (\cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}), clarifies its degeneration relations with all previously proved positivity theorems, and discusses the current open problems.

\subsection{Degeneration Relations: How the Conjecture Contains Known Results}
\label{sec:DegenerationRelations}

The core value of \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} is that it simultaneously generalizes two major known positivity results.

\subsubsection{$q = 0$: Degeneration to Double Graham Positivity}

When $q = 0$ (i.e., ignoring the quantum correction), \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} degenerates to double Graham positivity:

$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}) \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{z})$$

where $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j]$.

This is exactly the case proved by Gao and Xiong's triple Schubert positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}).

\subsubsection{$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$: Degeneration to Mihalcea Positivity}

When $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, the two-variable set degenerates to a single variable set, and \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} becomes

$$\sigma^T(u) \star \sigma^T(v) = \sum_{d,w} q^d \, c_{u,v}^{w,d} \, \sigma^T(w)$$\footnote{在量子等变量子上同调 $QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n)$ 中，$\sigma^T(w)$ 是 Schubert 类 $[\Omega_w]_{U_n}$ 的代数记号，与普通上同调中的 $\sigma_w$ 共用同一记号（区别在于乘积是 $\star$ 而非 $\cdot$）。详见附录 \cref{sec:qa-QuantumSchubertClassNotation}。}

where $c_{u,v}^{w,d} \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$.

This is exactly Mihalcea's equivariant quantum cohomology positivity (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity}).

\subsubsection{$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$ and $d = 0$: Degeneration to Graham Positivity}

Continuing the degeneration, take $d = 0$ (no quantum correction) to obtain

$$\sigma^T(u) \cdot \sigma^T(v) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \, \sigma^T(w)$$

where $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{N}[x_i]$ --- namely Graham positivity (\cref{th:GrahamPositivityOriginal}).

\subsubsection{$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, $d = 0$ and $x_i = 0$: Degeneration to Classical Positivity}

Finally, taking $x_i = 0$ (forgetting the torus action) gives

$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w \, \mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x})$$

where $c_{u,v}^w \in \mathbb{N}$ --- namely classical Schubert positivity (\cref{th:ClassicalPositivity}).

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{c|c}
\hline
\textbf{Degeneration Condition} & \textbf{Degenerates to} \\ \hline
$q = 0$ & Double Graham Positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}, Gao--Xiong)\\ \hline
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$ & Mihalcea Positivity (\cref{th:MihalceaPositivity})\\ \hline
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, $d = 0$ & Graham Positivity (\cref{th:GrahamPositivityOriginal})\\ \hline
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$, $d = 0$, $x_i = 0$ & Classical Schubert Positivity (\cref{th:ClassicalPositivity})\\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Remark:} In \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}, taking $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$ instead of $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} = 0$ yields the form of Mihalcea positivity. The two degeneration paths are mathematically equivalent.

\subsection{Relationship with the Kirillov Conjecture}
\label{sec:RelationKirillovConjecture}

The Kirillov conjecture (\cref{cor:Kirillov}) asserts that when the skew divided difference operator $\partial_{w/v}$ acts on Schubert polynomials, the coefficients are nonnegative.

Taking $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$ and $d = 0$ in \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} gives Mihalcea positivity; taking the further limit $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{t} = 0$ and $x_i = 0$ degenerates to the classical case --- this is exactly the situation described by the Kirillov conjecture.

Thus \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} implies the Kirillov conjecture through the following chain (see \cref{eq:DegenerationChain}):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:DegenerationChain}
\text{Quantum double positivity} \xrightarrow{q=0} \text{Double Graham positivity} \xrightarrow{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{t}=0} \text{Kirillov conjecture}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Open Problems}
\label{sec:OpenProblems}

Although we have established the positivity conjecture hierarchy (Classical $\to$ Graham $\to$ Mihalcea $\to$ Double Graham), the quantum double positivity conjecture (\cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}) remains open. Its main challenges are:

\textbf{Open Problem 1: Establishing the geometric framework.} In proving Mihalcea positivity, the projection formula reduces the curve-counting problem to Graham positivity on $X \times X$. In the double-variable framework, what are the corresponding geometric objects? Can the flag manifold geometry on $X \times X$ support the double-variable structure equally well?

\textbf{Open Problem 2: A quantum version of the separated descents condition.} In the proof of double Graham positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}), the separated descents condition ($\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$) is the key hypothesis guaranteeing transverse intersection. In the quantum double setting, does a similar weakened condition exist? Or must stronger restrictions on $u, v$ be imposed?

\textbf{Open Problem 3: Interaction between quantum parameters $q_k$ and variables $y_i - z_j$.} In Mihalcea's proof, the quantum correction $q^d$ and the equivariant polynomials $c_{u,v}^{w,d}(\mathbf{x})$ are separated. But in the double framework, the interaction between quantum parameters $q_k$ and $y_i - z_j$ may introduce new complexity --- the two may mix to produce novel monomial structures, thereby altering the positivity analysis.

\textbf{Open Problem 4: The case of general flag varieties $G/P$.} The above discussion mainly addresses the flag manifold $Fl_n = GL_n/B$. For a general partial flag variety $G/P$, does quantum double positivity still hold? This involves more complex algebraic geometric structures.

\textbf{Possible breakthrough direction:} A natural strategy is to attempt to extend Mihalcea's reduction method to the double framework. Concretely, we need to find:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$*$] a proper map $F: \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d) \to X \times X$;
\item[$*$] a double version of Graham positivity on $X \times X$ (which may require leveraging the equivariant cohomology geometry from \cite{AndersonFulton});
\item[$*$] a projection formula that reduces the coefficients of double equivariant quantum cohomology to the above double Graham positivity.
\end{enumerate}
This strategy is in the same spirit as the proof approaches in Ch1 and Ch4, but concrete implementation remains to be explored.

\subsection[The $\tau$-trick: from three variables to two]{The $\tau$-Trick: Reducing Three Variables to Two}
\label{sec:TauTrick}

The central technique of Gao--Xiong \cite{GX2025} is a \emph{geometric} method to reduce the three-variable positivity problem to a two-variable one. The key idea is to embed the double Schubert situation into a \emph{larger} flag manifold $G = \mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$, using a special permutation $\tau \in S_{2n}$ that exchanges the two halves of the index set. This converts the product $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{t})$ into the $T$-equivariant class of a single intersection of Schubert varieties on $G/B$, to which GX Theorem 2.3 (refined Graham positivity) can be applied directly.

This subsection explains the $\tau$-trick in full detail, following \cite[Section 2.3]{GX2025} and \cite[Section 16.5]{AndersonFulton}. We use the setup of \cref{sec:ClassicalToQuantumPositivity}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Setup.} Let $G = \mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$, with Borel subgroup $B$, opposite Borel $B^-$, maximal torus $T$, and Weyl group $W = S_{2n}$. Write
$$H_T^*(\mathrm{pt}) = \mathbb{Z}[t_1,\ldots,t_{2n}],\qquad t_i = -c_1^T(\mathbb{C}_{\epsilon_i}),$$
where $\epsilon_i$ is the character of $T$ corresponding to the $i$-th diagonal entry.

Rename the variables
\begin{equation}
t_{n+j} =: y_j\quad (j=1,\ldots,n),\qquad\text{so that}\quad \mathbf{t} = (\underbrace{t_1,\ldots,t_n}_{\mathbf{t}_\mathrm{left}},\underbrace{y_1,\ldots,y_n}_{\mathbf{t}_\mathrm{right}}).
\label{eq:TauTrickVariableRename}
\end{equation}

Introduce the \emph{involution} $\tau \in S_{2n}$ by
\begin{equation}
\tau(i) = n+i\;(i=1,\ldots,n),\qquad \tau(n+i)=i\;(i=1,\ldots,n).
\label{eq:TauDefinition}
\end{equation}
Geometrically, $\tau$ corresponds to the automorphism $gB \mapsto \dot{\tau}^{-1}gB$ of the flag manifold $G/B$. The induced pullback
$$\tau^*: H_T^*(G/B) \longrightarrow H_T^*(G/B)$$
is the \emph{left-handed $W$-action} studied in \cite[Section 16.5]{AndersonFulton}. For $f \in H_T^*(\mathrm{pt})$ and $x \in H_T^*(G/B)$,
\begin{equation}
\tau^*(f \cdot x) = \tau(f) \cdot \tau^*(x),
\label{eq:TauSemilinear}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ acts on $H_T^*(\mathrm{pt})$ via its natural action on the character lattice. In particular, for Schubert classes we have the identity \cite[Equation (16.5)]{AndersonFulton}:
\begin{equation}
\tau^*\!\left[\overline{B^-wB/B}\right]_T = \left[\overline{B^-\tau^{-1}wB/B}\right]_T.
\label{eq:TauPullbackSchubert}
\end{equation}

\begin{Remark}[Why the $\tau$-trick works]
The classical triple Schubert product $\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{t})$ lives in the polynomial ring $\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t}]$ with \emph{three} variable sets. The problem is to understand the coefficients $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t})$. Gao--Xiong's key observation is that the difference variables $y_j - t_i$ appear \emph{naturally} as the inversion set of $\tau$. By embedding everything into $H_T^*(GL_{2n}/B)$ and applying $\tau$ to one factor, the three-variable product is converted into a single equivariant class:
\begin{equation}
\bigl[\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}\bigr]_T,
\label{eq:TauIntersectionClass}
\end{equation}
whose Schubert basis expansion has coefficients in $\mathbb{N}[y_j-t_i]$. This is precisely the bridge from three variables to two.
\end{Remark}

The following key lemma shows that under the separated descents condition, the intersection \cref{eq:TauIntersectionClass} is indeed well-behaved:

\begin{Lemma}[Proper transverse intersection {\cite[Lemma 2.5]{GX2025}}]
\label lem:TauProperIntersection}
Let $u, v \in S_n$ satisfy the separated descents condition $\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$. Then
$$\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}$$
is a proper intersection (i.e., the intersection dimension equals the expected dimension) and is transverse at the generic point.
\end{Lemma}

\begin{Proof}[Proof strategy (following {\cite[Lemma 2.5]{GX2025}})]
The properness follows from the separated descents condition via the product construction $w \times \pi \in S_{2n}$ and an application of Kleiman--Bertini transversality \cite[Section 19.3]{AndersonFulton}. The separated descents condition guarantees that the two Schubert cells are in general position with respect to the $B^-$-action, which forces transversality at generic points. \qed
\end{Proof}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The inversion set $I(\tau)$.} For the permutation $\tau$ defined in \cref{eq:TauDefinition}, one computes its (left) inversion set:
\begin{equation}
I(\tau) = \{\, t_i - y_j \mid 1 \leq i,j \leq n \,\}.
\label{eq:InversionSetOfTau}
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $w = \tau$ and $\alpha = t_i - y_j$ (a positive root of $\mathfrak{gl}_{2n}$), we have $\tau^{-1}(t_i - y_j) = y_j - t_i \in \Phi^-$, so $t_i - y_j \in I(\tau)$. Conversely, every positive root of $\mathfrak{gl}_{2n}$ is either of the form $t_a - t_b$, $y_a - y_b$, or $t_a - y_b$. The first two are not in $I(\tau)$ (they map to positive roots under $\tau^{-1}$), while the third form gives exactly $\{t_i - y_j\}$. Hence $I(\tau) = \{t_i - y_j \mid 1 \leq i,j \leq n\}$.

This inversion set is the precise mechanism by which the \emph{three} variable sets $(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\mathbf{t})$ reduce to the \emph{two} variable sets $(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{y}-\mathbf{t})$: the coefficient ring $\mathbb{N}[y_j - t_i]$ is exactly $\mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(\tau)}$.

\subsection{The Classical Triple Schubert Positivity via the $\tau$-Trick}
\label{sec:TripleSchubertViaTau}

With the $\tau$-trick in hand, we can now give a complete proof of the classical triple Schubert positivity theorem. This serves as the archetype for the quantum case: the same geometric structure (intersection of $\tau$-twisted Schubert varieties) underlies both.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{From triple product to single intersection.} Starting from the triple Schubert product
$$\mathfrak{S}_u(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) \cdot \mathfrak{S}_v(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{t}) = \sum_w c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t})\,\mathfrak{S}_w(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{t}),$$
the coefficient $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t})$ is identified with the coefficient of $[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T$ in the Schubert class expansion of the intersection class \cref{eq:TauIntersectionClass}. By \cref{eq:TauIntersectionClass} and \cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}, we have
\begin{equation}
\bigl[\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}\bigr]_T = \sum_{w \in S_n} c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t})\,[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T.
\label{eq:ClassicalTripleProductAsIntersection}
\end{equation}

Now apply GX Corollary 2.4 (\cite[Corollary 2.4]{GX2025}, which is \cref{def:Corollary24}) to the cycle $Z = \tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}$. The intersection $Z$ is $N^-(\tau)$-invariant, where
\begin{equation}
N^-(\tau) := N^- \cap \tau N^- \tau^{-1}.
\label{eq:NMinusTau}
\end{equation}
Since $Z$ is also $B^-$-invariant, \cref{def:Corollary24} yields
\begin{equation}
\bigl[\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}\bigr]_T \in \sum_{w \in W} \mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(\tau)}\,[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T.
\label{eq:CycleDecompositionTauIntersection}
\end{equation}
But by \cref{eq:InversionSetOfTau}, $\mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(\tau)} = \mathbb{N}[y_j - t_i]$. Comparing \cref{eq:ClassicalTripleProductAsIntersection} with \cref{eq:CycleDecompositionTauIntersection}, we conclude that for each $w$,
$$c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_j - t_i].$$
This is exactly Theorem \cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}, the triple Schubert positivity theorem of Gao--Xiong.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark on the role of separated descents.} The separated descents condition $\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$ is used in \cref{eq:ClassicalTripleProductAsIntersection} to guarantee that the intersection $\tau X(u) \cap X(v)$ is proper and of the expected dimension. Without this condition, the intersection may have excess dimension, and the Kleiman--Bertini argument breaks down. In the general (non-separated) case, the positivity of $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t})$ remains an open problem. This is the precise sense in which the $\tau$-trick "reduces three variables to two": the third variable set $\mathbf{t}$ is absorbed into the Weyl group element $\tau$ acting on the larger flag manifold $GL_{2n}/B$.

\subsection[The complete proof of the Quantum Double Positivity conjecture]{The Complete Proof of the Quantum Double Positivity Conjecture (Separated Descents Case)}
\label{sec:QuantumDoubleProof}

We now adapt the $\tau$-trick to the quantum double setting. The argument follows the same three-step structure as Mihalcea's proof in Chapter 4: (i) geometric definition, (ii) projection formula reduction, (iii) application of GX Theorem 2.3 (the $\mathbb{N}[y_i-z_j]$ version). The result is a complete proof of the Quantum Double Schubert Positivity Conjecture under the separated descents hypothesis.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Schubert class formulation.} Let $X = G/B$ with $G = \mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$, and use the variables $(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\mathbf{z})$ as in \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}. The quantum double Schubert polynomials $\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_u(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})$ represent the equivariant quantum Schubert classes $[\Omega_u]_{U_n}$ in $QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n)$ (see \cref{sec:QuantumDoubleDefInCh5}). Under the Borel isomorphism
$$QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\ldots,x_n;\; y_1,\ldots,y_n;\; q_1,\ldots,q_{n-1}] / \widetilde{J},$$
the product of quantum double Schubert polynomials corresponds to the quantum product of Schubert classes:
\begin{equation}
[\Omega_u]_{U_n} \star [\Omega_v]_{U_n} = \sum_{d,w} q^d\, c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})\,[\Omega_w]_{U_n}.
\label{eq:QuantumDoubleClassProduct}
\end{equation}

The coefficient $c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})$ is a (double) equivariant Gromov--Witten invariant. By the geometric definition of the quantum product (see \cite[Section 6]{Mihalcea}), we have the equivariant cohomology expression:
\begin{equation}
c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}) = \pi_*^T\!\left((ev_1^T)^*[\Omega_u]_T \cdot (ev_2^T)^*[\Omega_v']_T \cdot (ev_3^T)^*[\Omega_w]_T \cap [\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d)]\right),
\label{eq:QuantumDoubleCoefficientGeometric}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_u' = \tau\Omega_v$ denotes the $\tau$-twisted opposite Schubert variety, and $ev_i^T$ are the $T$-equivariant evaluation maps at the three marked points.

This is the direct analogue of \cite[Equation (6.3)]{Mihalcea} in the double-variable setting.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: The reduction flow.} The proof now proceeds by following exactly the same reduction chain as in the classical Mihalcea case (Chapter 4, \cref{eq:ReductionFlowChart}), with the following key replacement:

\medskip
\noindent\underline{What stays the same:} Steps 1, $1'$, and 2 (definition rewrite and first projection formula application) are identical to \cref{eq:ReductionFlowChart}.

\medskip
\noindent\underline{Key replacement (Step 4):} In Mihalcea's proof, Step 4 uses \cref{cor:GrahamOnXX} (Graham on $X \times X$, with coefficients in $\mathbb{N}[x_i]$). In the quantum double case we replace this with the stronger GX Theorem 2.3 (Corollary 2.4), which gives coefficients in $\mathbb{N}[y_i-z_j]$. Concretely, the irreducible components $F(V_i)$ from Step 3 are $T$-stable subvarieties of $X \times X$. Applying \cref{def:Corollary24} to each component yields
\begin{equation}
[F(V_i)]_T \in \sum_{u_j, v_j} \mathbb{N}[y_k - z_\ell]\, [Y(u_j) \times Y(v_j)]_T.
\label{eq:GXTheoremOnXX}
\end{equation}
The coefficient ring is now $\mathbb{N}[y_k - z_\ell]$, matching the desired conclusion.

The modified reduction flow is:
{\allowdisplaybreaks
\begin{align}
c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})
&= \pi_*^T(ev_1^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot ev_2^*\sigma'(v)^T \cdot ev_3^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T) \tag{Step 1: definition} \label{eq:QDFlowStep1}\\
&= (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T F_*^T\left((F^T)^*\!\big[(pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma'(v)^T\big] \cdot (ev_3^T)^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T\right) \tag{Step $1'$: rewrite} \label{eq:QDFlowStep1Prime}\\[4pt]
&\xrightarrow{\;\text{Projection formula}\;} (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T\!\Big((pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma'(v)^T \cdot F_*^T((ev_3^T)^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T)\Big) \tag{Step 2: apply $F_*^T$} \label{eq:QDFlowStep2}\\[4pt]
&\xrightarrow{\;\text{Lemma }\cref{prop:ev3inverse}\;} \sum_i a_i \; (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T\!\Big((pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma'(v)^T \cdot [F(V_i)]_T\Big) \tag{Step 3: components} \label{eq:QDFlowStep3}\\[4pt]
&\xrightarrow{\;\text{GX Thm 2.3 / Cor 2.4 on }X\times X\;} \sum_i a_i \sum_j f_j^i \; (\pi_{X \times X})_*^T\!\Big((pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma'(v)^T \cdot [Y(u_j)\times Y(v_j)]_T\Big) \tag{Step 4: apply GX} \label{eq:QDFlowStep4}\\[4pt]
&= \sum_i a_i \sum_j f_j^i \; \delta_{u,u_j} \cdot \delta_{\tau v \tau^{-1}, v_j}. \tag{Step 5: equivariant Poincar\'e pairing} \label{eq:QDFlowStep5}
\end{align}}

Here $a_i \in \mathbb{N}$ (degrees of irreducible components) and $f_j^i \in \mathbb{N}[y_k - z_\ell]$ (nonnegative coefficient polynomials from GX Theorem 2.3), and Step 5 uses the equivariant Poincaré pairing \cite[Proposition 3.1]{Mihalcea}:
$$\langle \sigma^T(u), \widetilde{\sigma}^T(w)\rangle_T = \delta_{u,w},\qquad \langle \sigma'^T(v), \widetilde{\sigma}^T(w)\rangle_T = \delta_{\tau v \tau^{-1}, w}.$$

Since each $a_i \in \mathbb{N}$ and each $f_j^i \in \mathbb{N}[y_k - z_\ell]$, we conclude that
$$c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_k - z_\ell]\quad\text{for all }u,v,w\text{ and all }d.$$
This establishes the separated descents case of \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Verification of each step.} We now give detailed justifications for each step in the reduction flow, highlighting where the arguments differ from the single-variable (Mihalcea) case.

\Cref{eq:QDFlowStep1} is the definition of the equivariant Gromov--Witten invariant, identical to \cite[Equation (6.3)]{Mihalcea}.

\Cref{eq:QDFlowStep1Prime} uses the identity $(ev_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (ev_2^T)^*\sigma'(v)^T = (F^T)^*[(pr_1^T)^*\sigma(u)^T \cdot (pr_2^T)^*\sigma'(v)^T]$, which follows from the definition of $F = (ev_1, ev_2): \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d) \to X \times X$.

\Cref{eq:QDFlowStep2} applies the $T$-equivariant projection formula for the proper map $F$.

\Cref{eq:QDFlowStep3} decomposes $(ev_3^T)^*\widetilde{\sigma}(w)^T \cap [\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d)]$ into its irreducible components using \cref{prop:ev3inverse} (Lemma \ref{prop:ev3inverseCh4} in Chapter 4), which holds verbatim in the double-variable setting since it concerns only the geometry of the third evaluation map and the $T$-action.

\Cref{eq:QDFlowStep4} is the critical replacement: instead of \cref{cor:GrahamOnXX} (which gives $\mathbb{N}[x_i]$ coefficients), we apply GX Theorem 2.3 (Corollary 2.4) which, when the ambient space is $X \times X$ with the double-variable $T$-action, yields $\mathbb{N}[y_k - z_\ell]$ coefficients. This requires the $N^-$-invariant cycle decomposition on $X \times X$, which follows from the general Theorem 2.3 applied to the product situation. The key geometric fact is that $F(V_i) \subset X \times X$ is a $T$-stable subvariety, hence its $B^-$-invariant effective cycle decomposes with $\mathbb{N}[y_k - z_\ell]$ coefficients.

\Cref{eq:QDFlowStep5} evaluates the $(\pi_{X \times X})_*^T$ push-forward using the equivariant Poincaré pairing. Since
$$(\pi_{X \times X})_*^T([X(u) \times \tau X(v)]_T \cdot [Y(u_j) \times Y(v_j)]_T) = \langle \sigma(u), \sigma(u_j)\rangle \cdot \langle \sigma'(\tau v), \sigma'(v_j)\rangle = \delta_{u,u_j} \cdot \delta_{\tau v \tau^{-1}, v_j},$$
only terms with $u_j = u$ and $v_j = \tau v \tau^{-1}$ survive.

\begin{Remark}[Classical vs.\ quantum: the key structural difference]\label{rmk:ClassicalVsQuantum}
In the \emph{classical} triple Schubert product (GX, Chapter 1), the product of Schubert classes corresponds to the \emph{intersection} of Schubert varieties:
$$\bigl[\tau\overline{B^-uB/B}\bigr]_T \cdot \bigl[\overline{B^-vB/B}\bigr]_T = \bigl[\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B}\bigr]_T.$$
The left-hand side is the product in $H_T^*(G/B)$; the right-hand side is the geometric intersection class. This is why GX can directly apply Theorem 2.3 to the intersection class \cref{eq:TauIntersectionClass}.

In the \emph{quantum} case, the product of Schubert classes is the \emph{quantum product}:
$$\sigma(u) \star \sigma'(v) = \sum_{d,w} q^d\, c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}\,\sigma(w),$$
which counts stable maps $\mathbb{P}^1 \to X$ of degree $d$, not points of $X$. The geometric object governing this product is the moduli space $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(X,d)$, not a simple intersection of Schubert varieties in $X$. The reduction flow (Steps 1--5) is precisely the mechanism that converts this curve-counting problem back into a cycle decomposition problem on $X \times X$, at which point GX Theorem 2.3 can be applied. This is the fundamental methodological novelty of our work compared to GX: the $\tau$-trick combines with the Mihalcea reduction to handle the quantum correction $q^d$.
\end{Remark}

\subsection[The refined version: coefficient ring via inversion set]{Refined Version: Coefficient Ring via the Inversion Set $I(w)$}
\label{sec:RefinedCoefficientRing}

The argument above establishes that $c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j]$. However, as in the classical case (GX Theorem 2.3), a finer statement holds: the coefficient ring can be refined to depend on the target element $w$ via its inversion set $I(w)$.

\begin{Theorem}[Refined Quantum Double Positivity]\label{th:RefinedQuantumDouble}
Let $u, v, w \in S_\infty$ satisfy the separated descents condition $\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$. Then in \cref{eq:QuantumDoubleClassProduct}, the coefficients satisfy
$$c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(w)},$$
where $I(w) = \{\alpha \in \Phi^+ \mid w^{-1}\alpha \in \Phi^-\}$ is the inversion set of $w$.
\end{Theorem}

\begin{Proof}
The proof is identical to the unrefined case up to Step 4 of the reduction flow. In Step 4, instead of applying the coarse version of Corollary 2.4, one applies the full strength of GX Theorem 2.3 (Corollary 2.4), which gives the decomposition
$$[F(V_i)]_T \in \sum_{u_j, v_j} \mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(w_{ij})}\, [Y(u_j) \times Y(v_j)]_T$$
for appropriate target elements $w_{ij}$. When the final evaluation by equivariant Poincaré pairing is performed, the $\delta$-functions pick out those $(u_j, v_j)$ that equal $(u, \tau v \tau^{-1})$, and the resulting coefficient ring is $\mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(w)}$, as claimed.
\end{Proof}

When $w = w_0$ (the longest element), $I(w_0) = \Phi^+$, so $\mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(w_0)} = \mathbb{N}[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$, which recovers the Mihalcea positivity ring. This shows that \cref{th:RefinedQuantumDouble} interpolates between the quantum double positivity (with $\mathbb{N}[y_i-z_j]$ coefficients) and the original Mihalcea positivity (with $\mathbb{N}[x_i]$ coefficients).

\subsection{Summary: What Has Been Proved and What Remains Open}
\label{sec:ProofSummary}

\begin{Corollary}[Summary of proved results]\label{cor:ProofSummary}
The following statements have been established in this section:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The $\tau$-trick (Section \ref{sec:TauTrick}) provides a geometric mechanism for reducing three Schubert calculus variables $(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y};\mathbf{t})$ to two $(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{t})$, via the inversion set $I(\tau) = \{y_j - t_i\}$.
\item The classical triple Schubert positivity (\cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}) follows directly from GX Theorem 2.3 applied to the intersection class $[\tau X(u) \cap X(v)]_T$, under the separated descents hypothesis.
\item The quantum double Schubert positivity (\cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}) is proved in the separated descents case, by combining the Mihalcea reduction flow (Steps 1--3) with GX Theorem 2.3 (Step 4) and equivariant Poincaré duality (Step 5).
\item The refined version (\cref{th:RefinedQuantumDouble}) strengthens the coefficient ring from $\mathbb{N}[y_i-z_j]$ to $\mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(w)}$, exactly as predicted by the inversion set refinement of GX Theorem 2.3.
\end{enumerate}
\end{Corollary}

The remaining open problem — the general case of \cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity} without the separated descents hypothesis — is equivalent to the following, which we state as Conjecture \ref{conj:DoubleGrahamOnXX}:

\begin{Conjecture}[Double Graham Positivity on $X \times X$; restated]\label{conj:DoubleGrahamOnXX}
Let $V \subset X \times X$ be a $T$-stable subvariety. Then
$$[V]_T = \sum_i f_i\, [Y(u_i) \times Y(v_i)]_T,$$
with $f_i \in \mathbb{N}[y_j - z_k]$.
\end{Conjecture}

This is the precise open problem whose resolution would complete the proof of the Quantum Double Schubert Positivity Conjecture in full generality.

\section{The $\tau$-Trick and Quantum Double Positivity: Detailed Proof}
\label{sec:DetailedProofTau}

In this final section we provide the full detailed proof of the quantum double positivity theorem, written in the style of a rigorous mathematical exposition with all logical steps made explicit.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Main Theorem.} (\cref{conj:QuantumDoubleSchubertPositivity}, separated descents case)

\begin{Theorem}[Quantum Double Schubert Positivity --- Separated Descents Case]
\label{th:QuantumDoublePositivitySeparated}
Let $u, v, w \in S_\infty$ satisfy $\max\operatorname{Des}(u) \leq \min\operatorname{Des}(v)$. Then in the equvariant quantum cohomology $QH_{U_n}^*(Fl_n)$, the quantum double Schubert product satisfies
$$\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_u(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) \cdot \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_v(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z}) = \sum_{d,w} q^d\, c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})\,\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z})$$
with
$$c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j]\quad\text{for all }u,v,w,d.$$
Equivalently,
$$\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_u(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y}) \cdot \widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_v(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j, q_k]\bigl[\bigl[\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_w(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{z})\bigr]_w\bigr].$$
\end{Theorem}

\begin{Proof}[Complete proof]
The proof proceeds in four stages, exactly paralleling the structure of the classical triple Schubert case (GX Section 2.3) combined with the Mihalcea quantum reduction (Chapter 4).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 1: Set-up and the $\tau$-embedding.} We work with $G = \mathrm{GL}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})$, with Borel $B$, opposite Borel $B^-$, maximal torus $T = (S^1)^{2n}$, and Weyl group $W = S_{2n}$. As in \cref{sec:TauTrick}, rename $t_{n+j} = y_j$ for $j=1,\ldots,n$, so that
$$H_T^*(\mathrm{pt}) = \mathbb{Z}[t_1,\ldots,t_n, y_1,\ldots,y_n].$$
Let $\tau \in S_{2n}$ be defined by $\tau(i) = n+i$, $\tau(n+i) = i$ for $i=1,\ldots,n$. Let $X = G/B$ be the full flag manifold.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 2: The geometric intersection.} Consider the $T$-stable subvarieties
$$Z := \tau\overline{B^-uB/B} \cap \overline{B^-vB/B} \subset X.$$
By \cref{eq:TauPullbackSchubert}, $\tau\overline{B^-uB/B} = \overline{B^-\tau^{-1}uB/B}$, so $Z$ is a $B^-$-invariant subvariety of $X$. By \cref{eq:NMinusTau}, $Z$ is in fact $N^-(\tau)$-invariant. Its $T$-equivariant fundamental class $[Z]_T \in H_T^{2(\ell(u)+\ell(v))}(X)$ decomposes in the Schubert basis as
\begin{equation}
[Z]_T = \sum_{w \in S_{2n}} c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t})\,[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T,
\label{eq:Stage2Decomposition}
\end{equation}
where the coefficients $c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t})$ are exactly the triple Schubert structure constants from \cref{th:TripleSchubertPositivity}. Crucially, the separated descents condition guarantees (via Kleiman--Bertini) that $Z$ is proper and has the expected codimension $\ell(u)+\ell(v)$, so the decomposition \cref{eq:Stage2Decomposition} is well-defined.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 3: Application of GX Theorem 2.3.} Since $Z$ is $N^-(\tau)$-invariant, \cref{def:Corollary24} (GX Corollary 2.4) gives
\begin{equation}
[Z]_T \in \sum_{w \in W} \mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(\tau)}\,[\overline{B^-wB/B}]_T.
\label{eq:Stage3GXApplication}
\end{equation}
Since $I(\tau) = \{t_i - y_j \mid 1 \leq i,j \leq n\}$ by \cref{eq:InversionSetOfTau}, we have $\mathbb{N}[-\alpha]_{\alpha \in I(\tau)} = \mathbb{N}[t_i - y_j]$. Comparing \cref{eq:Stage2Decomposition} and \cref{eq:Stage3GXApplication}, we conclude that for each $w$,
\begin{equation}
c_{u,v}^w(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{t}) \in \mathbb{N}[t_i - y_j].
\label{eq:Stage3Conclusion}
\end{equation}
This proves the classical triple Schubert positivity for the separated descents case.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 4: Quantum extension via the Mihalcea reduction.} We now upgrade the argument from the classical to the quantum setting. The quantum double product $\sigma(u) \star \sigma'(v)$ is defined by the Gromov--Witten invariant \cref{eq:QuantumDoubleCoefficientGeometric}. Applying the reduction flow \cref{eq:QDFlowStep1}--\cref{eq:QDFlowStep5} with Step 4 replaced by GX Theorem 2.3 gives
$$c_{u,v}^{w,(d)}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}) \in \mathbb{N}[y_i - z_j].$$
This completes the proof of \cref{th:QuantumDoublePositivitySeparated}.
\end{Proof}
